The formula tells us something that isn't obvious at all. Speed enters squared. Double the speed on the same circle, and the acceleration doesn't double. It becomes four times larger. For our particle, moving at eight metres per second instead of four would take thirty-two metres per second squared instead of eight. The radius sits underneath. Keep the speed fixed and double the radius, and the acceleration halves, to four metres per second squared. Why? On a wider circle, the object takes longer to turn through the same angle, so its velocity changes direction more gently. That's why the same speed can feel easy on a broad curve, but demanding on a sharp bend. Tight turns at high speed need the most acceleration of all.
