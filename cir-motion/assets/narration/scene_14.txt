Circular motion repeats. So we can ask two more questions about it. How long does one revolution take? That's the period, T, measured in seconds. And how many revolutions happen each second? That's the frequency, f, measured in hertz. One hertz is one cycle per second. They're reciprocals: f equals one over T, and T equals one over f. A shorter period means more turns every second. One revolution sweeps two pi radians in a time T. So the angular speed is two pi over T, which is two pi f. And one revolution covers two pi r of distance, so the speed is v equals two pi r over T. Back to our particle. Its period is two pi r over v: two pi times two, over four. Exactly pi seconds, about three point one four seconds. Its frequency is one over pi: about zero point three one eight hertz. And two pi times that frequency gives back two radians per second, just as before. Angular speed, period, frequency and linear speed aren't four separate facts. They're four ways of describing one motion.
