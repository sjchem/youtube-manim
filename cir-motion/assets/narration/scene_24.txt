Time to test your intuition. Five questions. Pause whenever you like. One. A ball circles on a smooth table, tied to a string. The string breaks. Does the ball fly outward, keep circling, or move along the tangent? Along the tangent, in a straight line. It needs a force to turn, not to keep moving. Two. An object goes round and round a circle at constant speed. Is its acceleration zero? No. Its velocity keeps changing direction, so it has centripetal acceleration. Three. A particle on a circle of fixed radius doubles its speed. What happens to its centripetal acceleration? It becomes four times larger, because it grows with v squared. Four. A satellite moves at constant speed in an ideal circular orbit. Is it accelerating? Yes. Gravity keeps turning its velocity, so it accelerates toward Earth's centre. Five. Can the angular acceleration be zero while the linear acceleration is not? Yes. In uniform circular motion, alpha is zero, but v squared over r is not. They measure different changes.
