Imagine a car on a perfectly straight road, holding a steady twenty metres per second. It isn't speeding up. It isn't slowing down. And it isn't changing direction. As far as physics is concerned, its acceleration is zero. Now put the same car on a circular track, still at exactly twenty metres per second. Nothing has changed on the speedometer. But watch the arrow of its velocity. It never stops turning. In physics, changing direction means changing velocity. And a changing velocity means acceleration. So here's the surprising conclusion. This car is accelerating the whole time, even though it never gets any faster. Why does that happen? Which way does the acceleration point? And what force makes it possible? To find out, we'll follow one idea from Episode one: velocity is a vector, and direction matters. This is Circular Motion.
