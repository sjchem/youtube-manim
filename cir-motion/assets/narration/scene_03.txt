Let's make that precise, using the vector tools from Episode one. Speed is a scalar. Velocity is a vector. And speed is simply the magnitude of the velocity: v equals the length of the vector v. Take two velocities. The first is five metres per second along the x axis: five i hat. The second is five metres per second along the y axis: five j hat. Both have a speed of five metres per second. But they are not the same vector. How much has the velocity changed? Subtract: delta v equals v two minus v one. Reverse the first arrow and add it tip to tail. Delta v is minus five i hat plus five j hat. Its length, by Pythagoras, is the square root of twenty-five plus twenty-five: about seven point zero seven metres per second. The speed didn't change at all. Yet the velocity changed by more than seven metres per second, just by turning. A change of direction alone can produce a real change in velocity. And that is exactly what happens, continuously, in circular motion.
