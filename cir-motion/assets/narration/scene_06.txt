Here's an idea from Episode one, revisited on a circle. Run once around a circular track of radius r. Your feet carry you the whole circumference: a distance of two pi r. Now watch the displacement: the arrow from where you started to where you are. It grows, swings round, and shrinks back to nothing. When you finish, you're standing exactly where you began. Zero displacement means zero average velocity over the lap: delta r over delta t is zero. Yet you were moving the whole time. Your average speed was two pi r over T, where T is the time for one lap. In uniform circular motion, that's also your constant speed. And zero average velocity says nothing about acceleration. At every instant during the lap, your velocity was turning. So instantaneous velocity points along the tangent, and in circular motion its direction never stops changing. Which brings us to the heart of this episode.
