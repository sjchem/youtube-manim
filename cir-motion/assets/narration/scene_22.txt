Now imagine the object travels around the circle while gradually going faster. Its direction is still changing, so it still needs a radial acceleration toward the centre: a r equals v squared over r. But its speed is changing too, and that needs a second component, along the tangent: a t equals d v d t. Speeding up, the tangential part points along the velocity. Slowing down, it points against it. On a fixed circle, the tangential acceleration is r times alpha, the angular acceleration. In full: a equals minus r omega squared r hat, plus r alpha theta hat. Here r hat points outward, and theta hat along the positive tangent. The two parts are perpendicular, so the total magnitude follows Pythagoras: the square root of a r squared plus a t squared. Our particle again. At four metres per second, on its two metre circle, the radial part is eight metres per second squared. Now give it an angular acceleration of zero point five radians per second squared. The tangential part is two times zero point five: one metre per second squared. Altogether, the square root of sixty-four plus one: about eight point zero six metres per second squared. The total arrow tips slightly forward of the centre. One part turns the velocity. The other stretches it.
