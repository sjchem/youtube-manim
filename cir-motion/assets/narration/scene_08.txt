How big is that acceleration? Let's derive it, rather than just state it. In a short time delta t, the object moves through a small angle, delta theta. The velocity is always perpendicular to the radius, so it turns through the same angle: delta theta. Here's a lovely way to see it. Collect every velocity arrow at one shared tail. Their tips trace out a circle of their own, with radius v. As the object goes round its circle once, the velocity arrow goes round this velocity circle once, in step. Two velocity arrows and delta v make an isosceles triangle: two sides of length v, with the angle delta theta between them. Split it down the middle. Each half is a right triangle, so delta v is two v sine of half delta theta. For small angles in radians, two sine of half the angle is almost exactly the angle itself. Watch the ratio as the angle shrinks: ninety degrees, forty-five, twenty, five. It closes in on one. So for a short interval, the size of delta v is v times delta theta.
