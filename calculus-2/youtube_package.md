## Description

A derivative measures how fast something changes. An integral measures how much has accumulated. They look like unrelated ideas — until the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus reveals they are opposite views of the same process. This film builds that connection visually: from a car's changing velocity and Riemann rectangles thinning into an integral, through the literal meaning of `dx`, a synchronized reveal of `A'(x) = f(x)`, a derivative shown as an entire new function, maxima and minima, a real fenced-garden optimization, the mystery of `+C`, substitution and integration by parts derived geometrically, the area between two curves, and a closing synthesis that ties position, velocity, and accumulated displacement into one picture.

Part 2 of a series. Part 1 is **From Limits to Derivatives**.
