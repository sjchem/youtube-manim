Here is the first ionization enthalpy for the first eighty-six elements, in order of atomic number. It rises and falls in a repeating pattern. The peaks are the noble gases: helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon. Closed shells hold on tightly. Right after every peak comes a cliff. Lithium, sodium, potassium, rubidium, caesium. One electron in a fresh shell, easy to remove. Turn the graph into a landscape, and the periodic pattern becomes a mountain range. Each climb is one period: ionization enthalpy generally rises across a row. And the peaks get lower as we go down. Larger atoms, more shielding, an easier escape. So ionization enthalpy generally increases across a period and decreases down a group: the mirror image of atomic size. But look closely at period two. The climb is not smooth. Two steps go the wrong way.
