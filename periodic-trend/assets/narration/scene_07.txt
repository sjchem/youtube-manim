Take a sodium atom. Eleven protons, eleven electrons. Its lone three s electron is loosely held. Give it enough energy, and it leaves. Sodium becomes a sodium ion, with a positive charge. Does it just become an atom with one less dot? No. The ion is dramatically smaller. One hundred and eighty-six picometres for the atom. Ninety-five for the ion. Why? The whole outer shell is gone. What remains is the compact, neon-like core, pulled by eleven protons with only ten electrons to hold. Now go the other way. Fluorine accepts an extra electron. The fluoride ion has nine protons, but ten electrons. Electron repulsion grows, and the same nuclear charge is shared among more electrons. So the cloud swells. Sixty-four picometres for the atom, one hundred and thirty-six for the ion. Atomic and ionic sizes are measured in different ways, so compare them as a pattern, not to the last picometre. Losing electrons shrinks. Gaining electrons swells.
