Here is a stranger comparison. Oxide, fluoride, sodium and magnesium ions. Count their electrons. Ten, ten, ten and ten. Exactly the same arrangement. Only the nucleus differs: eight, nine, eleven and twelve protons. Same electrons, more protons, stronger pull. Watch the clouds contract. One hundred and forty, one hundred and thirty-six, ninety-five, sixty-five picometres. The magnesium ion is less than half the size of the oxide ion, with the very same electrons. Even with the same number of electrons, the nucleus can completely change the size. Size tells us how close the electrons stay. Next question: how hard is it to pull one away?
