Inside a molecule, atoms share electrons. Here is hydrogen chloride. Draw the shared pair as an even cloud between the two atoms. That is a convenient first sketch, but not what happens. Chlorine pulls the shared electrons towards itself. The density shifts. Chlorine gains a small negative charge, hydrogen a small positive one. This tendency has a name: electronegativity. An atom's pull on the electrons it shares in a bond. Keep three ideas separate. Ionization enthalpy removes an electron from an isolated atom. Electron gain enthalpy adds one to an isolated atom. Electronegativity describes a tug of war for shared electrons, inside a bond. It is a relative scale, not a single measured energy. On Pauling's scale, hydrogen is about two point two, chlorine about three point two. The bigger the difference, the more lopsided the sharing.
