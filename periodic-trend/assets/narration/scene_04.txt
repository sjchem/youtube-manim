But an outer electron is never alone with the nucleus. Look at sodium. Eleven protons, and ten inner electrons, packed closer to the nucleus than the outer one. Those inner electrons repel the outer electron. They partly cancel the nucleus's pull. We call this shielding. So the outer electron does not feel all eleven protons. It feels a smaller, effective charge. We call it effective nuclear charge, Z effective: the positive charge an electron feels after shielding. Now watch the two effects compete. First, add protons while the new electrons join the same shell. Electrons in the same shell shield each other poorly. So each new proton wins. Z effective rises, and the cloud is pulled inward. Now add a new shell. The new outer electron starts farther out, outside a larger core of inner electrons. The extra protons are largely cancelled by the extra inner electrons, and distance takes over. The cloud expands. Charge pulls the cloud in. Distance and shielding let it spread out. Every trend in this video is a contest between them. Now we can solve our first mystery.
