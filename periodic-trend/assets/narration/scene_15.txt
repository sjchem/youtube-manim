Height now represents electron density. This landscape illustrates unequal sharing; it is not a calculated molecular orbital. Two identical atoms share evenly: a symmetric ridge. Now make one partner more electronegative. The ridge leans. Increase the difference, and the shared density slides further towards the stronger pull. Large differences suggest more ionic character. There is no universal cutoff between ionic and covalent bonding. Where are the strongest pullers? Light up the table by electronegativity. It generally increases across a period and up a group, towards the upper right. Fluorine sits at the top, with a Pauling value of about four. Caesium, at the lower left, is among the lowest. Same reason as before: a small atom with a high effective nuclear charge pulls bonding electrons closer. Now we know which atoms hold electrons tightly, and which pull on shared ones.
