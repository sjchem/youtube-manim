Fluorine or chlorine: which releases more energy when it gains an electron? Fluorine is smaller. Its nucleus is closer. Surely fluorine wins. Fluorine: minus three hundred and twenty-eight kilojoules per mole. Chlorine: minus three hundred and forty-nine. Chlorine releases more. Fluorine's five two p electrons occupy a compact region. The next electron must enter that same crowded region. A new electron must squeeze into that small space, and it is pushed back by strong electron-electron repulsion. Chlorine's three p region is larger. The newcomer has more room, so the repulsion is weaker. Both atoms attract the electron strongly. In fluorine, more of that attraction is cancelled by crowding. Size helps, but crowding matters too. That is why chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy of all the elements. But there is another way an atom can attract electrons, without completely gaining or losing one.
