This glowing cloud is a lithium atom: a tiny nucleus, wrapped in a haze of electrons. Now add one proton and one electron at a time. Beryllium. Boron. Carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine. The atom shrinks. We added particles, and it got smaller. Back to lithium. This time, move down the column. Sodium. Potassium. Rubidium. Caesium. Now the atom grows, and grows. Again, we added protons and electrons. So how can adding particles make an atom smaller in one direction, and larger in another? Hold on to that question. Its answer also explains why some atoms give electrons away so easily, while others pull electrons towards themselves. This is the story of periodic trends, and the invisible forces behind them.
