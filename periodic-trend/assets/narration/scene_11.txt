Beryllium: nine hundred kilojoules per mole. Boron: eight hundred and one. The trend says boron should be higher. The answer is in the orbitals. Beryllium's outer electrons fill two s. Boron's extra electron goes into two p, a higher-energy subshell. A two p electron is shielded a little more by the two s pair, and starts higher up in energy. So less energy is needed to remove it. The general trend is not broken. We simply removed a different kind of electron. The second surprise: nitrogen, fourteen hundred and two. Oxygen, thirteen hundred and fourteen. Nitrogen has three p electrons, one in each p orbital, all unpaired. Oxygen's fourth p electron must share an orbital with another electron. Two electrons in the same orbital repel each other strongly. That repulsion makes one of them easier to remove. So oxygen's first ionization enthalpy is a little lower than nitrogen's. Period three repeats both dips: magnesium to aluminium, and phosphorus to sulfur. The periodic trend isn't broken. We simply need a more detailed picture of the orbitals. Removing an electron is one experiment. Next, we reverse it.
