## Description

Add protons and electrons across the periodic table and atoms get *smaller*.
Add them down a column and atoms get *larger*. How can the same change do
opposite things?

In this episode we answer that with three questions — how far are the outer
electrons, how strongly does the nucleus pull on them, and how are they
arranged — and then use the same ideas to explain ionization enthalpy, its
famous exceptions (beryllium vs boron, nitrogen vs oxygen), electron gain
enthalpy (and why chlorine beats fluorine), electronegativity, and why sodium
and chlorine end up as salt. The film ends by turning the whole periodic table
into a 3D map of these invisible forces.

This is Part 4 of the Atomic Structure series and a direct sequel to
"Why Does the Periodic Table Have This Shape?".

Every number on screen comes from published data: NCERT Class XI Chemistry
(Unit 3) for atomic/ionic radii and electron gain enthalpies, the NIST Atomic
Spectra Database for ionization energies, Cordero et al. (2008) covalent radii
for the radius map, and Pauling electronegativities. Electron clouds are drawn
as probability, not orbits; schematic sizes and energy spacings are labelled on
screen.
