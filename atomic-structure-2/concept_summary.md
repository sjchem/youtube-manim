# Science and visual accuracy

The screenplay is original narration developed from the supplied treatment. The following sources support technical checks; no textbook passage is reproduced.

- **Bohr's numbers, as chapter 3 states them.** The radii `r_n = n^2 a0`, the speeds `v_n = alpha c / n` and the energies `E_n = -13.6/n^2 eV` are the fixed-nucleus hydrogen Bohr model, presented as the model's own claims before the film dismantles them. The displayed 0.53 Å, 2.12 Å and 2.19 × 10^6 m/s are rounded from `scipy.constants`. The 2n² capacity rule is stated there as school arithmetic and derived properly in chapter 12. [OpenStax: Bohr's Theory of the Hydrogen Atom](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/8-1-the-hydrogen-atom).
- **Matter waves and diffraction.** The amplitude drawing is not a physical wavy track. The worked electron example uses stated speeds of 1 and 2 million m/s and calculates wavelengths from h/(m_e v). The cricket-ball example is no longer qualitative: 0.160 kg at 40 m/s gives 1.04 × 10^-34 m, which the logarithmic ruler places nineteen decades below a nucleus. The ruler's logarithmic axis is labelled as such on screen. [OpenStax: Wave–Particle Duality](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/6-6-wave-particle-duality).
- **The two-slit chapter.** Equal Gaussian far-field amplitudes generate the coherent and distinguishable-path profiles using the same envelope. The detector-state overlap weights the cross term. Source pulses and screen detections do not draw a classical electron route; new detections belong to a fresh run. Physical path information removes fringes, independently of a human reading the record. [Feynman Lectures III, chapter 1](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/III_01.html).
- **The circumference identity.** Chapter 6's claim is exact rather than approximate. Bohr's `m v r = n hbar` is algebraically equivalent to `2 pi r = n lambda` with `lambda = h/mv`, so the de Broglie wavelength of the first-shell electron equals the first orbit's circumference identically — 3.32492 × 10^-10 m either way. The audit pins the ratio to 1 within 1e-9. The film says ring closure recovers Bohr's postulate within this constrained model; it does not claim de Broglie derived the Bohr model in general, and the ring is retired as an analogy in the same chapter.
- **Uncertainty.** Standard deviations concern repeated preparations, not merely instrument error. The demonstrated Gaussian reaches the lower bound; general states need not. Chapter 7 shows Heisenberg's microscope because most viewers arrive with it, and then explicitly withdraws it as an explanation: the bound is a property of the state, and the wave-packet argument that follows involves no measurement at all. [OpenStax: The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/7-2-the-heisenberg-uncertainty-principle).
- **Hydrogen wave functions and nodes.** Spatial orbitals are solutions, not literal tracks or containers. Our numerical radial functions are normalized, 2s has its node at `r = 2 a0`, and the real 2pz state has a nodal xy plane. [MIT: Hydrogen Atom Wavefunctions](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/pages/unit-i-the-atom/lecture-6-hydrogen-atom-wavefunctions-orbitals/).
- **Probability density versus radial distribution.** These answer different questions and the film draws both. `|psi_1s|^2` is *largest at the nucleus*; the radial distribution `P(r) = r^2 R_{n,l}(r)^2` is *zero* there, because a shell of vanishing radius encloses no volume, and it peaks at exactly `a0`. Treatments that draw a standing wave with zero amplitude at the nucleus and call that the probability of finding the electron there are conflating the two, and chapter 11 is written to avoid it. The `4 pi r^2 |psi|^2` form is equivalent only for s states; `r^2 R^2` is used because it is correct for every `l`. The peak for the largest `l` of each shell lands on `n^2 a0`, which is Bohr's radius for that shell.
- **State labels and real orbitals.** `ml` labels angular-momentum projection. The usual real `px` and `py` functions are linear combinations of magnetic eigenstates, and so are four of the five real d functions; only `dz2` is a magnetic eigenstate on its own. The film names the five d orientations — `dxy`, `dxz`, `dyz`, `dx2-y2`, `dz2` — because a viewer will meet those names again, but it never assigns an individual `ml` value to `px`, `py` or to the four combination d shapes. The renderer's signed real-basis index is kept out of those pedagogical labels. [MIT: Hydrogen Atom lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-61-physical-chemistry-fall-2007/c2078ff25c61c136e67ceb6fd8eb9b34_lecture21to22.pdf).
- **Exclusion.** Two opposite-spin occupations per spatial orbital follow from fermionic state restrictions, not an electrostatic two-ball story. Chapter 14 derives the need for a fourth label rather than asserting it: helium's two 1s electrons share `n`, `l` and `ml`, so if no two electrons may share every label, the spin projection is the only one left to differ. That establishes where the label must come from; it is not a proof of the spin-statistics theorem. [OpenStax: The Exclusion Principle and the Periodic Table](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/8-4-the-exclusion-principle-and-the-periodic-table).
- **Hund's first rule.** Carbon shows the preference for separate orbitals while a choice remains; oxygen shows the first forced pairing. The preference is attributed to electron interactions and quantum symmetry, not to a bare "electrons repel so they spread out" story. Because the three 2p orbitals are degenerate in the isolated atom, which one takes oxygen's pair is not determined.
- **Screening, penetration and the filling order.** Chapter 13 shows the three third-shell radial distributions on one axis. The innermost lobes sit at 0.74, 3.00 and 9.00 `a0` for 3s, 3p and 3d, so only 3s has appreciable density inside the screening core; integrated out to 1.5 `a0` the ordering is 3s > 3p > 3d by more than an order of magnitude at each step. Beyond about 2 `a0` that ordering no longer holds, and the film's core band is drawn at the width where the claim is true. The configuration diagram is an approximation, energy spacing is schematic, and the order depends on the atom, configuration and ion. [OpenStax: Electronic Structure of Atoms](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-atoms-first-2e/pages/3-4-electronic-structure-of-atoms-electron-configurations).
- **Madelung's rule.** Chapter 17 states it as increasing `n + l` with ties broken by smaller `n`, shows the 2p/3s tie explicitly, and applies it to potassium, whose ground configuration is `[Ar] 4s1`. The film calls it a very good guide to neutral-atom ground configurations rather than a law, and names the exceptions as the sequel's subject. The rule orders *filling*, not the orbital energies of any particular atom; in neutral transition metals 3d lies below 4s once occupied. [NIST: Potassium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/potassiumtable1.htm).
- **Deferred to the sequel.** The archived Cr/Cu chapter discusses the ground configurations `[Ar]3d5 4s1` for Cr and `[Ar]3d10 4s1` for Cu. [NIST: Chromium](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/chromiumtable1_a.htm), [NIST: Copper](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/coppertable1.htm).

The treatment's phrase “only three extra letters” was omitted: ORBITAL adds two letters to ORBIT. Its suggested direct orientation mapping was refined to avoid assigning real px/py orbitals individually to ml = −1/+1. “Higher energy means more intricate” is also not used as a universal rule: ideal hydrogen has different shapes at the same n and energy.

Probability density is not a point probability. `P(V)` integrates the density over a region. A mathematically exact nodal surface has zero density; the visual does not claim a finite forbidden layer of thickness. The smooth map in Scene 9 is a cross-sectional visualization, while the accumulated points are projections of sampled three-dimensional detections.

Nuclear icons and orbital sizes are enlarged or rescaled for legibility. The ring diagrams in chapters 3 and 16 space their shells evenly rather than as `n^2`, which is a drawing choice the narration does not contradict. Neutral neon/sodium comparisons use schematic density views rather than quantitatively computed many-electron charge densities. Sodium ionization is an illustrative occupancy change, not a simulation of a specific reaction.

## Analogies, and where each one stops

Four analogies are used, and each is captioned as an analogy on screen in the chapter that uses it.

- **The ring (chapter 6)** earns one idea: a restriction on a wave selects discrete patterns. Real states are three-dimensional and the electron has no track.
- **The clamped string (chapter 8)** supplies a question, not a model. An atom has no string and no rigid wall.
- **The cat (chapter 9)** carries the accumulation idea: one observation is a point, only the pile is a distribution. It stops precisely where it matters — a cat has a definite position between photographs, and an electron's position is not a property waiting to be revealed. The narration says so as the analogy leaves.
- **The street of houses (chapter 12)** carries the counting: a house has as many floors as its number, a floor has an odd number of rooms, `1 + 3 + 5 + ... = n^2`, and a room takes two. It does not claim that a shell is a container or that a floor is a place, and the chapter says both out loud.

## The three linked equations and worked examples

- **Scene 4:** `lambda = h/p`, with `p ≈ m_e v`. An electron at `1.0 × 10^6 m/s` gives approximately `0.727 nm`; doubling the speed gives `0.364 nm`. A cricket ball of `0.160 kg` at `40 m/s` gives `1.04 × 10^-34 m`. Bohr's `v_1 = 2.1877 × 10^6 m/s` gives `3.3249 Å`. The nonrelativistic assumption is valid at all these speeds. The on-screen numbers and wave spacing share one tracker.
- **Scene 6:** `2 pi r = n lambda` with `lambda = h/(m v)` rearranges to `m v r = n h / (2 pi)`, which is Bohr's postulate. At `n = 1` both sides evaluate to `3.32492 × 10^-10 m`.
- **Scene 7:** `p = hbar k`, `k = 2 pi/lambda` links the spread in wave numbers to momentum: `Delta x Delta k ≥ 1/2` becomes `Delta x Delta p ≥ hbar/2`. Bohr's `r_1` and `v_1` give `p_1 ≈ 1.993 × 10^-24 kg m/s`, a momentum magnitude; the full classical trajectory additionally specifies exact position and momentum vectors, which the quantum state cannot do. At `Delta x = 100 pm`, the minimum momentum spread is approximately `5.27 × 10^-25 kg m/s`; at `50 pm`, it is approximately `1.05 × 10^-24 kg m/s` (shown as `10.55 × 10^-25` so the on-screen unit stays fixed). The readouts follow the same width tracker as the packet. The Gaussian reaches the lower bound. The two plots use separate axes and rescaled peak heights. Standard-deviation brackets run from the mean to +sigma, so their lengths represent one standard deviation rather than the full ±sigma interval.
- **Scene 8:** expand `H psi = E psi` as `[-hbar²/(2m_e) nabla² + V(r)] psi = E psi`, with Coulomb `V(r) = -e²/(4 pi epsilon_0 r)`. For the fixed-nucleus nonrelativistic hydrogen model, `E_n ≈ -13.6/n² eV`, so 1s and 2p give -13.6 and -3.40 eV. No claim is made that n alone controls energies in many-electron atoms. [OpenStax: Schrödinger equation](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/7-3-the-schrodinger-equation), [OpenStax: Hydrogen atom](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/8-1-the-hydrogen-atom).
- **Scene 11:** `P(r) = r^2 R_{n,l}(r)^2`, normalized to one, peaking at `a0` for 1s and at `n^2 a0` for the largest `l` of each shell.
- **Scene 12:** `sum_{l=0}^{n-1} (2l+1) = n^2` orbitals per shell, and `2n^2` electrons.
- **Scene 17:** `n + l` for the first ten subshells, with the `2p`/`3s` tie at three broken by the smaller `n`, and `4s` at four filling before `3d` at five.

`utils/quantum_examples.py` calculates the SI values from SciPy's physical constants. `utils/audit.py` additionally applies the atomic-unit Hamiltonian by finite differences to the actual 1s/2p wave functions, checks the radial distribution's normalization and peak positions, checks the penetration ordering inside the core, and checks the Madelung sequence against the order the film draws. Uncertainty is not presented as a derivation of Schrödinger's equation: the latter supplies dynamics and the allowed stationary states, which uncertainty alone does not determine.
