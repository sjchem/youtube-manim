# Concept Summary — Complete Visual Trigonometry

## Main question

How can one rotating point explain right triangles, coordinates, waves, inverse functions, identities, general triangles, sound, Fourier analysis, and Euler’s formula?

## Core idea

Trigonometry is the mathematics of rotation viewed through projections:

1. An angle measures a turn.
2. Cartesian coordinates record the rotating point’s horizontal and vertical positions.
3. Those perpendicular projections form a right triangle.
4. Similar triangles make side ratios depend only on the angle.
5. A radius of one turns the ratios into unit-circle coordinates.
6. Recording a coordinate through angle or time creates a wave.
7. Inverse functions run the projection backward.
8. Identities express distance, reciprocal views, and composed rotations.
9. Altitudes extend right-triangle reasoning to every triangle.
10. Fourier analysis combines many simple rotations.
11. Euler’s formula writes a rotation as a complex exponential.

## Learning outcomes

After watching, a beginner should be able to explain:

- Cartesian coordinates, quadrants, degrees, and radians;
- the Pythagorean theorem and why similar-triangle ratios stay fixed;
- SOH–CAH–TOA and all six trigonometric functions;
- exact values at 0, 30, 45, 60, and 90 degrees;
- unit-circle coordinates and quadrant signs;
- sine and cosine graphs, period, amplitude, frequency, phase, and shift;
- the meaning and principal branches of arcsine, arccosine, and arctangent;
- reciprocal, quotient, Pythagorean, addition, double-angle, and half-angle identities;
- when to use the laws of sines and cosines;
- why pure tones are sinusoidal and how Fourier components build richer signals;
- why Euler’s formula is another representation of unit-circle rotation.

## Accuracy boundaries

- Special-angle values are derived from 45–45–90 and 30–60–90 triangles.
- Inverse trig functions return principal values; complete equations may have additional solutions.
- The law-of-sines SSA configuration can produce zero, one, or two triangles.
- The Fourier sequence is a conceptual square-wave approximation; the visible overshoot near jumps is expected Gibbs behavior.
- The film does not claim every physical wave is literally circular motion. Uniform circular projection produces a sinusoid, and sinusoids are powerful components for modeling signals.
- Euler’s formula is presented geometrically; a rigorous derivation from power series or differential equations lies beyond this introductory course.
