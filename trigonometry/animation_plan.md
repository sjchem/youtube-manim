# Animation Plan — Complete Visual Trigonometry Course

Target duration: **45:15** · Frame: **16:9** · Audience: school students and math-anxious adult learners

## Continuity design

- Treat all seventeen chapters as one film.
- Preserve one living Oceanic background through every boundary.
- Keep the golden moving point as the conceptual guide: cabin → coordinate → triangle endpoint → unit-circle point → graph point → speaker signal → Fourier component → complex number.
- Use short corner handoffs, not repeated centered chapter titles.
- Reserve large typography for the hook, major identities, final thesis, and subscribe card.
- Keep narration waits visually alive with moving trackers, redraws, subtle focus pulses, or drifting background elements.

## Act I — Build the language

### Chapter 01 · Scene 01 · Repetition leads to trigonometry · 0:00–1:35

- Place a heartbeat, a pendulum, and a sound signal side by side and reveal that all three repeat.
- Draw repetition through time as the same wave, then follow that wave backward into a circle and a right triangle.
- Welcome the audience to Data Alchemist and promise a visual journey they can remember.
- Transfer a Ferris-wheel cabin’s height to a live sine trace, establishing the central question before terminology.

### Chapter 02 · Scene 02 · Angles, degrees, and radians · 1:35–3:05

- Animate an angle as a turn.
- Compare degrees with a radius wrapped onto the circumference.
- Derive one full turn as (360^\circ=2\pi) radians.

### Chapter 03 · Scene 03 · Coordinates and Pythagoras · 3:05–6:05

- Move one live point through all four Cartesian quadrants.
- Carry its horizontal and vertical projections with it.
- Convert the coordinate displacement into a 3–4–5 triangle.
- Make (9+16=25) visible as areas before generalizing to (a^2+b^2=c^2).

### Chapter 04 · Scene 04 · Invariant ratios · 6:05–8:25

- Scale a right triangle while its ratios remain fixed.
- Name sine, cosine, and tangent.
- Change only the angle and update values live.

### Chapter 05 · Scene 05 · All six functions · 8:25–11:25

- Expand SOH–CAH–TOA over a color-coded triangle.
- Flip the three ratios to create cosecant, secant, and cotangent.
- Organize all six functions around sine and cosine.

### Chapter 06 · Scene 06 · Special angles · 11:25–15:25

- Cut a square diagonally to derive the 45–45–90 triangle.
- Bisect an equilateral triangle to derive the 30–60–90 triangle.
- Construct the exact-value table from geometry.
- Carry the exact points onto the first quadrant of the circle.

## Act II — Let the circle take over

### Chapter 07 · Scene 07 · Unit circle · 15:25–17:30

- Normalize the hypotenuse to one.
- Map the triangle’s projections to ((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)).
- Rotate through all quadrants and derive the central Pythagorean identity.

### Chapter 08 · Scene 08 · Sine and cosine graphs · 17:30–20:15

- Transfer circular height to the graph in real time.
- Predict quarter-turn landmarks.
- Compare sine and cosine and reveal periodicity.

### Chapter 09 · Scene 09 · Wave controls · 20:15–22:10

- Animate (A\sin(B\theta+C)+D) one parameter at a time.
- Translate symbols into radius, rotation speed, starting angle, and axle height.

### Chapter 10 · Scene 10 · Inverse trigonometry · 22:10–25:40

- Reverse angle-to-ratio arrows.
- Show why a repeated sine graph requires a principal branch.
- Contrast inverse sine with reciprocal sine.
- Give arcsine, arccosine, and arctangent distinct visual questions.

### Chapter 11 · Scene 11 · Identity toolkit · 25:40–30:40

- Open with an intimidating formula wall, then collapse it into three visual moves: resize, combine, and reuse or reverse.
- Rescale one unit-circle triangle to generate the tangent–secant and cotangent–cosecant Pythagorean identities.
- Resolve a second rotation along and sideways to the first ray, then read the vertical and horizontal components to build the addition formulas.
- Reverse the second turn for subtraction, repeat an equal turn for double angles, and run that step backward for half angles.
- End with a three-card tool chooser so students select a transformation instead of searching memory for a formula.

### Chapter 12 · Scene 12 · Non-right triangles · 30:40–34:40

- Drop an altitude into an oblique triangle.
- Equate two expressions for the shared height to build the law of sines.
- Use projections and Pythagoras to build the law of cosines.
- Finish with a visual tool chooser and the area formula.

## Act III — Where trigonometry lives

### Chapter 13 · Scene 13 · Sound · 34:40–36:30

- Drive a speaker cone with a sine signal.
- Combine harmonic components into a richer waveform.

### Chapter 14 · Scene 14 · Fourier analysis · 36:30–41:00

- Separate a rich signal into individual frequencies.
- Replace the time graph with an amplitude spectrum.
- Add odd harmonics progressively to approximate a square wave.
- Return to the interpretation of harmonics as simple rotations.

### Chapter 15 · Scene 15 · Euler’s formula · 41:00–44:00

- Introduce the complex plane as a two-dimensional number line.
- Follow a rotating unit vector and its real and imaginary projections.
- Reveal (e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta).
- Set (\theta=\pi) to produce Euler’s identity geometrically.

### Chapter 16 · Scene 16 · Final synthesis · 44:00–45:00

- Reassemble the complete dependency chain.
- End on “Many formulas. One moving idea.”

### Chapter 17 · Scene 17 · Subscribe · 45:00–45:15

- Deliver a concise call to action and fade the complete background.

## Semantic color system

- Gold: angle, active point, phase, final focus
- Cyan: sine, vertical projection, waves, normal signal
- Green: cosine, horizontal projection, confirmed result
- Orange: tangent, frequency, inverse slope
- Purple: abstraction, phase, complex or limiting behavior
- Red: undefined expressions and warnings
- White: central radius, hypotenuse, and main equations
- Muted gray: axes, construction guides, secondary structure
