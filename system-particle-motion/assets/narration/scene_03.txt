Take two particles on a light rod. Where would you hold the rod, so that it balances? If the masses are equal, the answer is easy: exactly halfway between them. Now make one of them three times heavier. Hold the rod at the middle, and it tips. The balance point has moved toward the heavier mass. In uniform gravity, the balance point is the centre of mass. For two particles on a line, it's a weighted average of their positions. x c m equals m one x one, plus m two x two, all divided by m one plus m two. Each position is weighted by its mass. A heavier particle pulls the average toward itself. Let's try it. A two kilogram mass sits at zero. A six kilogram mass sits at four metres. Two times zero, plus six times four, is twenty-four. Divide by the total mass, eight kilograms, and the centre of mass is at three metres. One metre from the heavy mass, three metres from the light one. The distances are in the inverse ratio of the masses.
