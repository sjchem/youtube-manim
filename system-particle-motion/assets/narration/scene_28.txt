A solid cylinder and a thin ring, with the same mass and the same radius, are released together at the top of a ramp. Both roll without slipping. Which reaches the bottom first? Pause, and make your prediction. Here's the race. The cylinder wins, clearly. Energy explains why. Both lose the same potential energy. But the ring must put half its kinetic energy into spinning, the cylinder only a third. So the cylinder has more left over for moving forward. Static friction supplies the turning torque. With enough grip, it prevents slipping and does no work at the stationary contact point. The acceleration is g sine beta, divided by one plus I c m over M R squared. For the cylinder: two thirds g sine beta. For the ring: one half g sine beta. On this thirty degree ramp, that's about three point two seven, versus two point four five metres per second squared. And a block sliding down a frictionless ramp, with no rotation at all, would beat them both, at g sine beta: four point nine metres per second squared. Mass and radius cancel out. The distribution of mass decides the race. Centre of mass, torque, moment of inertia and energy, all on one ramp.
