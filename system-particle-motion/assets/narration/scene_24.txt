Linear momentum, m v, describes translation. What describes rotation? Choose an origin fixed in an inertial frame. A particle's angular momentum about it is L equals r cross p: position cross momentum. Its size is r p sine theta. Like torque, it's momentum times a lever arm. Even a particle moving in a straight line has angular momentum about a point beside its path. And if no force acts, the lever arm stays the same, so L stays constant. For a particle moving on a circle around the origin, the velocity is perpendicular to the radius. So L equals m r v. And since v equals r omega, L equals m r squared omega. Now a rigid body. Add up m r squared omega for all its particles, and we get I omega. For a body turning about a fixed symmetry axis, L equals I omega, pointing along the axis. For a general body on a fixed axis, only the component of L along that axis equals I omega. The full vector can tilt. The bearings then supply a sideways torque to keep the axis fixed. About that same fixed origin, the net external torque equals the rate of change of total angular momentum, when internal torques cancel. Tau equals d L by d t. Along a fixed axis, with I constant, the rate of change of axial angular momentum is I alpha. So tau equals I alpha follows. Force changes linear momentum. Torque changes angular momentum.
