[warm, conversational delivery] So a rolling body carries two kinds of kinetic energy. Its centre of mass moves: one half M v c m squared. And it rotates about its centre of mass: one half I c m omega squared. Rolling without slipping, omega equals v c m over R. Substitute, and factor out one half M v c m squared. K equals one half M v c m squared, times one plus I c m over M R squared. [short pause] [emphasized] That bracket decides how the energy is shared. For a solid cylinder, I over M R squared is one half, so one third of its kinetic energy is rotation. For a ring, it's one, so half of its energy is rotation. [pause] [curious] Same mass, same radius, same speed, but different shares of energy. That has a surprising consequence on a slope.
