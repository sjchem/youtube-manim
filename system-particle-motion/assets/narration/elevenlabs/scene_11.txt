[warm, conversational delivery] Why is a door easier to open at the handle than near its hinges? [short pause] Push near the handle, and the door swings easily. Push with the same force near the hinges, and it barely moves. The turning effect of a force is called torque, or moment of force. And it depends on more than the force alone. Torque is r cross F. Here r is the position of the point where the force acts, measured from the axis, and F is the force. Its size is r F sine theta, where theta is the angle between r and F. [pause] [curious] There's another way to see it. Extend the force into a line, its line of action. The perpendicular distance from the axis to that line is the lever arm. Torque equals F times the lever arm. [short pause] [emphasized] Push straight toward the hinge, and the line of action passes right through the axis. The lever arm is zero. That push cannot start the door turning from rest. [pause] Let's put numbers on it. Push perpendicular to the door with twenty newtons, zero point eight metres from the hinges. The torque is twenty times zero point eight: sixteen newton metres. Same push, only zero point two metres from the hinges: four newton metres. The same force gives four times less torque, because the lever arm is four times shorter.
