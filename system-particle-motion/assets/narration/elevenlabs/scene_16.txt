[short pause] [curious] Imagine gathering all of a body's mass into a thin ring around the axis, at a distance k. Choose k so the moment of inertia stays the same. That distance is the radius of gyration. I equals M k squared. So k equals the square root of I over M. For a thin ring, the mass is already at the rim. k equals R. For a solid disc, I is one half M R squared. So k is R over root two: about zero point seven one R. [pause] The mass isn't really there. The radius of gyration is a bookkeeping distance. But it tells you at a glance that the disc's mass sits closer to the axis than the ring's.
