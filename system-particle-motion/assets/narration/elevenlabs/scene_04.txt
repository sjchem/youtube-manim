[warm, conversational delivery] Is the centre of mass always at the geometric centre of an object? For a uniform, symmetric body, it is. A uniform sphere, a uniform disc, a uniform straight rod: their centres of mass sit right at their centres. Symmetry does the work. For every bit of mass on one side, there is a matching bit on the other. [short pause] But spread the mass unevenly, like a hammer with a heavy iron head, and the centre of mass shifts toward the heavy end. [short pause] [curious] It can even lie where there is no material at all. A uniform ring has its centre of mass at the centre of the hole, in empty space. So the centre of mass isn't decided by geometry alone. It's decided by how the mass is distributed. [pause] [curious] Now toss the hammer. It tumbles in a complicated way. But watch only the centre of mass. Ignoring air resistance, it traces a smooth parabola, just like a thrown ball. [short pause] [warmly] Hold on to that. It's a clue to why the centre of mass matters so much.
