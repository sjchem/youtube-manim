[warm, conversational delivery] In fixed-axis rotation, every particle shares the same omega and alpha. But do they share the same linear motion? [short pause] [emphasized] No. A particle at perpendicular distance r from the axis moves with speed r omega. Farther out means faster. When the spin speeds up, each particle also gains speed along its path. That's the tangential acceleration: a t equals r alpha. And because each particle moves on a circle, it also needs an inward, radial acceleration: a r equals r omega squared. [short pause] The tangential part changes the speed. The radial part changes the direction. The two parts are perpendicular. Add them as vectors. Their magnitudes form a right triangle, so Pythagoras gives the total acceleration. [pause] [curious] As the wheel spins faster, the radial part grows like omega squared, and soon dominates. One rigid body, one omega. But each particle has its own speed and its own acceleration, set by its distance from the axis.
