Let's not start with the fifth power. Start small, and watch what happens. a plus b to the power zero is just one. One term, coefficient one. To the first power, it's a plus b itself. Two terms, each with coefficient one. Now square it. Let a and b be positive lengths making up this square's side. Split each side into a and b. The square breaks into four regions. A square of side a: that's a squared. A square of side b: b squared. And two rectangles, each a by b. One stands up, one lies down, but their areas are equal: a times b. Slide them together. Two copies of a b. So a plus b, squared, is a squared, plus two a b, plus b squared. Coefficients one, two, one. The algebra agrees. Every term in the first bracket meets every term in the second. a times a. a times b. b times a. b times b. Four products. But a b and b a are the same product, just reordered. So they merge. That two isn't a rule to memorise. It counts something: two different ways to end up with one a and one b. Hold on to that idea. A coefficient counts the ways.
