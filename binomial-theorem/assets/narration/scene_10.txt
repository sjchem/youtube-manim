Turn the triangle into a little maze. Start at the top. At every row, step down to the left, or down to the right. Call a left step a, and a right step b. Five steps down, and you reach row five. Your path is a list of five letters, a's and b's, just like our choice machines. Here's one path: a, b, a, a, b. It ends two places from the left, because exactly two steps went right. Where you land depends only on how many b's you took. Not on their order. So how many paths reach this spot, the ten? Any path with two right steps among its five. That's five choose two. Watch them arrive. All ten paths, layered on top of each other. Every number in the triangle counts the paths that reach it. And Pascal's rule appears a third time. Every path into this spot comes through one of the two spots above it. Four paths arrive from the upper left. Six from the upper right. Four plus six, ten. Paths, choices, coefficients. Three different stories, and the same numbers in every one. Pascal's triangle, combinations, binomial coefficients. Not three facts that happen to agree, but one structure seen from three sides. Now let's make the walk random.
