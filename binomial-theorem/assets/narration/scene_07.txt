Here it is: the binomial theorem. a plus b to the n equals the sum, for k from zero to n, of n choose k, a to the n minus k, b to the k. Read it from the inside out. b to the k: k of the brackets chose b. a to the n minus k: the rest chose a. n choose k: the number of ways to decide which brackets those are. And the sum: collect every possible number of b's, from none at all to every bracket. For n equal to zero, one, two, and so on: choose, count, collect. Let's test it. Set n to five, and let k run from zero to five. Each value of k gives one term: five choose k, times a to the five minus k, times b to the k. Evaluate each count. One, five, ten, ten, five, one. The expansion we started with, rebuilt without multiplying anything. And we never needed the triangle. The counts come straight from choosing. For power ten, the middle term is ten choose five: two hundred fifty-two a to the fifth b to the fifth. But look closer at the exponents. They move in a very particular way.
