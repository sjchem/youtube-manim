## Description

Throw a ball at 20 metres per second, at 45 degrees. Where will it be one
second later? How high will it climb? How far will it go?

Episode 01 of Visualized Physics — From Motion to the Quantum World builds the
whole answer from the ground up. Scalars and vectors. Distance versus
displacement. Adding and subtracting vectors with the triangle and
parallelogram laws (and a delivery drone). Scalar multiplication, unit
vectors, and the most useful idea in the episode: resolving a vector into
components. Then position, velocity and acceleration, the constant-acceleration
equations — and finally the flight itself, in 3D: the ball's shadow on the
floor glides steadily while its shadow on the wall only rises and falls.

Watch the flight become a curve in space-time, and see why the path must be a
parabola. Derive the time of flight (2.89 s), maximum height (10.20 m) and
range (40.82 m), discover why 45° goes farthest — and when it doesn't (air
resistance, a launch from a ledge). Five conceptual challenges at the end.

Assumptions: g = 9.8 m/s², constant; no air resistance except where shown; the
ball lands at its launch height unless stated. The drag example models a
light foam ball and is illustrative.

Sources: OpenStax University Physics Volume 1; Lichtenberg & Wills, Am. J.
Phys. 46, 546 (1978); World Athletics Track and Field Facilities Manual.
