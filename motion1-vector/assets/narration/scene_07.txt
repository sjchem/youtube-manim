Picture a delivery drone. It flies six metres east. Then eight metres north. Is there a single straight flight that would have taken it from the same start to the same finish? Yes. That's the resultant displacement. This is the triangle law: tail of the second vector on the head of the first. The resultant points from the starting point to the final point. Or place both vectors at the same starting point, and complete a parallelogram. Its diagonal is the same resultant. That's the parallelogram law. The two methods always agree. Now let the angle between the two vectors change. Call it phi. When they point the same way, the lengths simply add. When they point opposite ways, they subtract. In between, the law of cosines gives the length: R equals the square root of A squared, plus B squared, plus two A B cosine phi. Check the extremes. Phi equals zero: cosine is one, and R is A plus B. Phi equals one hundred and eighty degrees: cosine is minus one, and R is the absolute difference. And when the vectors are perpendicular, phi is ninety degrees, cosine of ninety is zero, and the middle term vanishes. R equals the square root of A squared plus B squared. It's the Pythagorean theorem, now doing vector addition. Back to our drone. Six squared is thirty-six. Eight squared is sixty-four. Together, one hundred. The square root of one hundred: a resultant of ten metres. Its direction: the inverse tangent of eight over six, about fifty-three point one three degrees north of east. Two separate flights, replaced by one arrow: ten metres, at fifty-three point one three degrees. Adding vectors tells us their combined effect. Next: what does it mean to subtract them?
