Finally, the big one. How far does the ball travel? To travel far, a projectile needs two things: enough horizontal speed, and enough time in the air. Horizontal velocity is constant, so the range is just v zero cosine theta, times the flight time. Substitute T equals two v zero sine theta over g. Now use a trig identity: two sine theta cosine theta equals sine two theta. And the range equation appears: R equals v zero squared sine two theta, over g. For our throw: twenty squared times sine ninety degrees, over nine point eight. About forty point eight two metres. Check the picture: fourteen point one four metres per second, for two point eight nine seconds, gives approximately that range. We've rounded both values. A shallow launch gives plenty of horizontal speed, but the ball comes down quickly. A steep launch keeps the ball up for a long time, but it creeps forward slowly. The range depends on how those two effects combine. So which angle wins?
