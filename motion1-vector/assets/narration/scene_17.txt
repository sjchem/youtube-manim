If we know where an object starts, how fast it's moving, and how it accelerates, can we predict where it will be? For constant acceleration, yes. Start in one dimension. Velocity changes by the same amount every second. So v equals v zero plus a t. On a velocity-time graph, that's a straight line. The signed area gives displacement; below the axis counts negatively. A rectangle, v zero times t, plus a triangle, one half a t squared. So x equals x zero plus v zero t plus one half a t squared. Combine the two, eliminating time, and we get a third: v squared equals v zero squared plus two a, x minus x zero. These three equations hold whenever the acceleration along that axis is constant. Now the leap to two dimensions. We don't need any new equations. We write the same equations twice: once for x, once for y. x of t uses the horizontal parts: x zero, v zero x, a x. y of t uses the vertical ones: y zero, v zero y, a y. Calculate the horizontal motion. Calculate the vertical motion. Then put them together to find the full position. With that, we're ready to return to our original question: how does a flying ball move?
