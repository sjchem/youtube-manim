Once the ball leaves your hand, what controls its motion? In our model, we ignore air resistance. Only gravity acts after launch in this ideal model. Near Earth's surface, gravity gives everything the same downward acceleration, about nine point eight metres per second squared. We treat it as constant, because a thrown ball never rises far enough for gravity to weaken noticeably. Choose x pointing right and y pointing up. Then the acceleration vector is minus g j hat. Notice what that means. There's no horizontal acceleration at all. a x is zero. And the vertical acceleration is constant: a y is minus g. Look at the gravity arrow as the ball flies. Rising, at the top, falling: it never changes. So two different motions happen at once: steady horizontal velocity, and steadily changing vertical velocity. Both share the same clock.
