A unit vector has a length of exactly one. It describes a direction, and nothing else. i hat is one unit along x. j hat is one unit along y. Any vector in the plane is A x copies of i hat, plus A y copies of j hat. We can also find the unit vector along any arrow that isn't zero. Divide the vector by its own length. Take A equals three i hat plus four j hat. Its length is five. Divide by five: A hat is three-fifths i hat plus four-fifths j hat. It lands on the unit circle, pointing the same way. Now magnitude and direction are stored separately. Five tells us how much. A hat tells us which way. Multiply them back together, and the original vector reappears. Every direction you can point to is a spot on this circle. Scalar multiplication changes a vector's size. Unit vectors pin down its direction. Together they let us write any vector as a magnitude times a direction. And that brings us to the most useful idea in this whole episode.
