[warm, conversational delivery] [short pause] [curious] What happens when we multiply a vector by an ordinary number, a scalar k? If k is greater than one, the vector stretches. Same direction, longer arrow. If k is between zero and one, it shrinks. Still the same direction. If k is negative, the arrow flips around, and its length is multiplied by the size of k. And if k is exactly zero, we get the zero vector: no length, and no single direction. With numbers: A is three i hat plus four j hat. Two A is six i hat plus eight j hat. Every component doubles, so the length doubles. Minus A is minus three i hat, minus four j hat. Same length, opposite direction. This simple rule is everywhere in physics. Newton's second law says the net force equals mass times acceleration. For positive mass, a nonzero net force points in the same direction as acceleration. Push a cart. Double the mass, and the same acceleration needs twice the force. The arrows grow, but they never turn.
