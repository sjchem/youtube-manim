[warm, conversational delivery] Before we describe movement, we need a reference point and a set of axes: a coordinate system. An object's position is then a vector from the origin: r equals x i hat plus y j hat. As the object moves, the position vector follows it. Between two moments, the change in position is the displacement: r final minus r initial. In components: x final minus x initial, and y final minus y initial. Here's a subtle point. Move the origin somewhere else. Every position vector changes. But the displacement, the arrow from the first point to the second, stays exactly the same. Shift the origin while keeping the axes fixed: positions change, but displacement doesn't. Think of two people giving directions from different street corners. They'd describe your location differently, but agree on your start-to-finish displacement, using the same axes. Displacement tells us how position changed. What it doesn't tell us is how quickly the change happened. [pause] [warmly] For that, we need velocity.
