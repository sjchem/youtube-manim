[warm, conversational delivery] A vector carries two pieces of information at once: a magnitude, and a direction. We draw it as an arrow. The length shows how much. The way it points shows where. Displacement, velocity, acceleration, force and momentum are vectors. A nonzero vector points in a definite direction. In writing, a small arrow on top marks a vector: A with an arrow. Its magnitude, the length of the arrow alone, is written between two bars. There's one more rule that makes something a true vector: vectors combine by vector addition, arrow after arrow, not number plus number. On a flat grid, any arrow can be described by how far it reaches across, and how far it reaches up. A x along i hat, plus A y along j hat. We'll unpack this soon. For now, notice: one vector can describe movement in two directions at once. Back to our car. Moving at twenty metres per second is a speed. Moving east at twenty metres per second is a velocity. That difference looks small. But it changes the mathematics of motion completely. [pause] [warmly] And the first place we see it is the difference between distance and displacement.
