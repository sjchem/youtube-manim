[warm, conversational delivery] Average velocity is displacement divided by the time it took: delta r over delta t. Follow a car on a winding road. Over a long interval, the average velocity points along this chord, straight from start to finish. Shrink the time interval. The chord gets shorter, and swings closer to the road itself. As the interval approaches zero, the displacement per second approaches the tangent velocity: d r d t, the derivative of position with respect to time. So the instantaneous velocity always points along the path, tangent to the direction of motion. Like any vector, it has components: v x is d x d t, v y is d y d t. And its length, the square root of v x squared plus v y squared, is the speed. That's the difference in one line. Speed tells you how fast. Velocity tells you how fast, and in which direction. Now drive around a roundabout with the speedometer locked at the same value. The speed never changes. But the velocity arrow keeps turning. So the velocity is changing all the time, even at constant speed. Velocity connects position and time. And because it's a vector, its direction matters just as much as its size.
