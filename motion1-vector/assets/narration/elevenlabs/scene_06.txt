[warm, conversational delivery] [short pause] [curious] When several movements happen one after another, what is their combined effect? That's what vector addition answers. R, the resultant, equals A plus B. Walk five metres east, then five more metres east. Your displacement: ten metres east. Now walk five metres east, then five metres west. You've walked ten metres, but your displacement is zero. Same distances. Very different results. The difference is direction. Between those extremes, two vectors can partly reinforce, and partly cancel. So we can't simply add their lengths. Place the tail of B at the head of A. The resultant runs from the very start to the very end. Swap the order: B first, then A. You arrive at exactly the same point. A plus B equals B plus A. Vector addition is commutative. With three vectors, you can group them either way. A plus B first, then C. Or B plus C first, then A. The same final arrow. Vector addition is associative. So order and grouping don't matter. Only the arrows themselves do. [short pause] [curious] But how do we calculate the resultant's length and direction? Geometry gives us two equivalent tools.
