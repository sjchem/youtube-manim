Here are ten counters, numbered one through ten. That is our universe for this puzzle. We will sort them by two rules: prime and even. A prime is a whole number greater than one with exactly two positive divisors, one and itself. An even number is divisible by two. Three, five, and seven belong on the prime side. Four, six, eight, and ten belong on the even side. One and nine belong to neither. We have left one counter waiting: the number two. Where should it go? Two is prime, because its positive divisors are one and two. It is also even, so it belongs in the overlap. In fact, every larger even number has two as an additional divisor, which is why two is the only even prime. The intersection is the set containing two. Its cardinality, or number of members, is one. The vertical bars mean that we are counting members. For comparison, the union contains eight distinct numbers. It includes everything that passes either rule, counting two just once. The result can be read in two ways. Prime intersect even contains only two. Prime union even has eight members. The braces tell us which elements belong; the vertical bars tell us how many there are.
