We are ready to return to our two friends. One asks for no meat; the other asks for no dairy. This time, the available menu has eight toppings. Mozzarella and ricotta are dairy. Bacon and sausage are meat. Mushroom, olive, onion, and pepper are neither. Choose the toppings that meet both friends' requests before we reveal them. Start with everything we need to exclude. Dairy or meat gives the union of the two unwanted collections. Now keep the rest of the available menu. That means taking the complement of the union. The cheese and meat disappear, leaving four toppings for our pizza: mushroom, olive, onion, and pepper. We removed the combined selection. There is another way to check the same result. Take a mushroom and ask whether it is outside the dairy set. Yes. Then ask whether it is outside the meat set. Yes again. The other three surviving toppings pass both exclusions too. One route excludes the union. The other keeps both complements’ shared members. These are two recipes for exactly the same selection, which brings us to a famous mathematical law. Call the shareable toppings S. We can calculate S by taking the available menu U and removing dairy union meat. We can also calculate S by intersecting not dairy with not meat. Next, we will prove why those two descriptions always agree.
