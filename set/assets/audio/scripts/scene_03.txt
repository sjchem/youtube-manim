Imagine a small animal park. Every animal we are considering belongs to the park's collection. Within it, we can select the mammals: the cat, tiger, dog, and whale. Inside the mammals, we can select the cat family, which includes both the domestic cat and the tiger. Every animal in this smallest collection is also in the middle collection and the largest one. That relationship is called being a subset. To make the nesting easier to see, imagine three open lunchboxes standing in for the three collections. The smallest sits inside the middle one, and both sit inside the largest. Drop our cat into the smallest box. It has landed inside all three at once. The boxes model membership; mathematical sets do not need physical walls. Now compare the two statements. One cat is an element of the mammals set. The entire cat-family set is a subset of mammals. Membership talks about an element; the subset relation talks about all the elements of a set. Can we reverse the second statement? Is every mammal a cat? The dog settles it. A dog is a mammal and is not a cat. One counterexample is enough to disprove that reversed subset claim. Here is the mathematical chain: the cat family C is a subset of mammals M, and M is a subset of the animal universe U. If x belongs to C, then x must belong to M. That is what the subset symbol promises.
