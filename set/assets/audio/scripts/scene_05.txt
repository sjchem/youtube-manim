Back at the animal park, we have two properties to organise. One habitat represents mammals. The other represents animals of egg-laying kinds. The dog and whale belong on the mammal side. The chicken and the snake shown here belong on the egg-laying side. Our snake represents an egg-laying kind; not every snake species lays eggs. So far, separate habitats seem to work. Then the platypus arrives. It is a mammal, and platypuses also lay eggs. Where should it go? Give yourself a moment to decide. Putting it only on one side would hide the other membership. Instead, bring the two boundaries together. Their overlap makes room for both properties, and the platypus can sit inside both without being copied. This is the useful idea behind a Venn diagram. The boundaries show relationships between the rules. The shared region says that both rules are true. Our animal has one identity and two memberships. Now let us take that idea back to two friends choosing their pizza toppings. We can now state the rule without naming an animal. An element x belongs to the intersection of M and E exactly when x belongs to M and belongs to E. The platypus gave us one concrete x.
