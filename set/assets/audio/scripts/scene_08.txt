Look at the toppings available in this kitchen. Our six choices are mozzarella, ricotta, mushroom, olive, onion, and pepper. This particular collection is our universe. Now select the dairy toppings. Mozzarella and ricotta go together. If we want everything in our universe that is not dairy, we keep the other four. That selection is the complement of the dairy set. We write a small c after the set's name. The phrase “not dairy” hides a question: among which objects? Let us tilt the kitchen shelf into three dimensions and look beyond it. There may be apples, bananas, and pizza elsewhere, but they were not part of the six toppings we agreed to consider. They do not automatically enter the answer. The universe tells us the collection from which we are choosing. A universe need not be finite, but we must specify it. Now keep the dairy set unchanged, but restrict the universe to those dairy toppings themselves. What belongs to the universe while being outside the dairy set? Nothing. The complement is now empty, even though the dairy set stayed the same. Complement means not in the selected set, but still in the universe. The general equation makes our shelf precise: the complement of D is U minus D. An element x belongs to the complement only when x is in U and x is not in D. Both conditions matter.
