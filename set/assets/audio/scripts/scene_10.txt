The two pizzas have the same toppings, even though we described their selection in different ways. Let us give those recipes a diagram. A and B can now be any two subsets of the same universe. The first recipe begins with their union, everything inside either boundary. Taking the complement of that entire selection leaves the region outside both. That is the complement of A union B. The second recipe starts with the complement of A, everything in the universe outside A. Then consider the complement of B, everything outside B. To pass both exclusions, an element must belong to both complements. Their intersection selects the region outside both original sets. It is the same region we found with the first recipe. Imagine cutting those two selections into transparent stencils. We can separate them in space, tilt them, and slide one onto the other. Their shapes line up. This is De Morgan's first law: the complement of a union equals the intersection of the complements. Neither dairy nor meat means not dairy and not meat. Logic uses the same pattern. Negating the whole statement “p or q” gives “not p and not q.” The whole statement is what the parentheses group together. Finally, follow one arbitrary element x. Being in the complement of A union B means x is outside A and outside B. That is exactly what it means for x to belong to both complements. Since this works for every x in U, the two sets are equal.
