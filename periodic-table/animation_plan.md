# Scene plan — The Periodic Table, Explained Visually

The story follows one question: where can the next electron go? Part 2 supplies the orbital vocabulary; Part 3 turns those states into the table and then into chemistry. Sixteen scenes share the same tile, orbital-box and colour system. Planned length: 22:43.

The screen carries the subject, a short heading, and the current count or configuration. The narration carries the explanation and qualifications. Overview element symbols are reference detail; close-up tiles and diagrams carry the teaching. Lato replaces serif titles, outlines are restrained, and background bubbles are faint and static. Static narration holds stay steady; orbital shapes and purposeful camera moves provide motion.

| 08 | 11:00 | Li/Na/K covalent-size illustrations lose one electron; common ionization equation; water reaction bubbles. Contrast closed outer s/p sets in Ne/Ar/Kr. | Similar valence structure gives related chemistry; ionization costs energy; noble gases are usually unreactive. |

| 10 | 14:12 | A readable 14-subshell sequence, n+l scores, diagonal chart, then Fe/Fe²⁺ configurations and the 6s→4f connection. | Madelung is a shortcut for neutral ground configurations; energies change, and transition-metal ions lose 4s first. |

| 12 | 17:13 | Colour all four blocks, retain helium's s colour, raise a capacity skyline and show one representative angular shape per family; return to the flat table. | Shapes represent families; counts set the widths and explain the early gaps and long strips. |
| 13 | 18:36 | Sweep Li→Ne, Na→Ar, K→Kr; show repeated chemistry and PERIODIC; count row lengths. Reveal 2s+2p, 4s+3d+4p, and 6s+4f+5d+6p recipes. | Periods are recurring valence cycles, not the full filling of one principal shell. |
| 14 | 20:01 | Period-two spheres shrink on one covalent-radius scale; group-one spheres grow; element labels retain their size. Short arrows connect size, ionization and metallic character. | Effective attraction and extra shells explain broad trends, with exceptions and radius-definition limits in narration. |
| 15 | 21:28 | Reverse the table into blocks, orbitals and four capacities; refill and rebuild; finish over a 3D skyline with orbitals→configurations→table→chemistry. | Nature supplies the patterns; chemists choose a useful way to draw the map. |
| 16 | 22:33 | Channel subscribe card and four gently rotating orbital shapes. | Invite the viewer to the periodic-trends continuation. |

The five signature reveals are the capacity-to-column transformation, electron-by-electron period two, the potassium/3d sequence, the physical extraction of the f strips, and the final reverse reconstruction.
