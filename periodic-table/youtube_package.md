## Description

Why two columns on the left, ten in the middle, six on the right — and fourteen
elements exiled underneath? The widths reveal the allowed electron states. In this video we rebuild the
table from its quantum ingredients, and see why the blocks have these sizes
and why the familiar drawing folds the longest rows underneath.

This is Part 3 of the Atomic Structure series. Part 2 turned orbits into
orbitals; here those orbitals pay off: s, p, d and f orbitals come in 1, 3, 5
and 7 independent orbitals, each holding two electrons — 2, 6, 10 and 14. Those are the
widths of the table.

Notes on accuracy: the n + l (Madelung) rule is a guide for neutral atoms, with
known exceptions such as chromium and copper (both shown). Atom sizes are
covalent radii from Cordero et al. (2008), drawn to scale. Orbital shapes are
polar plots of the angular probability density. The f block is placed La–Yb /
Ac–No with group 3 = Sc, Y, Lu, Lr, using the Sc–Y–Lu–Lr layout convention; other tables draw that boundary differently.
