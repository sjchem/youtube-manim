Lithium, sodium and potassium differ in size. Each has a closed core and one outer s electron. That outer electron is the key. Compared with the core electrons, it is much easier to remove. Removing it still costs energy. During a reaction, other energy changes can make the complete process favourable. Each atom can therefore form a positive ion by giving up one electron. We write M becoming M plus and an electron. Three different metals share this common change. For example, each reacts with water to release hydrogen gas. Under comparable conditions, the reaction becomes more vigorous from lithium to sodium to potassium. Now compare the noble gases on the other side of the table. Their closed outer set is n s two, n p six. Removing an electron is difficult, and adding one cannot simply fill a vacancy in that closed set. They are usually unreactive, although heavier noble gases can form compounds. Repeating valence patterns give related chemistry. The same counting idea also explains the long middle rows.
