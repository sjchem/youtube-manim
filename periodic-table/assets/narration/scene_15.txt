Why does the periodic table have this strange shape? Now we can answer. Trace the diagram back to its building blocks. Four blocks. Four orbital families. And four capacities: two, six, ten and fourteen. Run the story forward. Orbitals hold electrons. Their occupations form configurations. Valence patterns recur, and related elements line up in columns. What looked like an awkward outline now has a physical explanation. Begin with electron states, and the block widths and repeating rows follow. Atomic number sets the sequence. Recurring valence structure shapes the groups. Subshell capacities set the widths. Our compact drawing folds the long rows underneath. The periodic table is a map of the quantum structure of atoms. We choose how to draw the map; nature supplies the patterns. From orbitals to configurations, from configurations to the table, and from the table to chemistry.
