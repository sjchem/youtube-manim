This is one of the most recognizable pictures in science: the periodic table. But look at its outline. Why does it have this strange shape? Two columns on the left. Ten in the middle. Six on the right. And two rows of fourteen positions tucked underneath. Why separate them? Why these widths? And why do elements directly above each other often share similar chemistry? There is a pattern hiding here. Two, six, ten, fourteen. Those numbers come from the states we met in Part Two: s, p, d and f. Follow where electrons can go, and we can rebuild the table. Its layout reveals the quantum structure of atoms.
