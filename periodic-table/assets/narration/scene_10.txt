Follow the filling sequence. Each principal shell does not have to finish before another begins. One s, two s, two p, three s, three p. Then four s, three d and four p. Later, six s brings us towards four f. A useful shortcut predicts much of this sequence: n plus l, or Madelung's rule. n is the principal shell number. l labels the family: zero for s, one for p, two for d, three for f. Four s gives four plus zero: four. Three d gives three plus two: five. Take the lower score first. If scores tie, the smaller n comes first. In this grid, subshells with the same n plus l score lie along a diagonal. Follow each diagonal towards increasing n, then move to the next score. The familiar sequence appears. Treat this as a guide to neutral ground configurations. The real atom minimizes total energy, and some configurations differ. Four s and three d energies change with occupancy. Transition-metal ions usually lose four s electrons first. The same counting guide next brings four f into the picture, in period six. Four f holds fourteen electrons.
