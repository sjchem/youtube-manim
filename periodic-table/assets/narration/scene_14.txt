The same electron structure also helps explain broad trends in properties. Let us start with the size of atoms. Compare lithium through fluorine across period two. Each step adds a proton and an electron. The added electron enters the same principal outer shell. The inner core remains similar, while electrons in that outer shell screen each other incompletely. The effective nuclear attraction generally increases. The electron distribution contracts. The atoms become smaller. Using covalent radii, lithium is a little over twice the size of fluorine. Now compare the alkali metals down group one. A new outer shell opens. More inner electrons shield the nucleus. The atoms grow. On this covalent-radius scale, caesium is almost twice the size of lithium. This stronger or weaker attraction also influences how much energy it takes to remove an electron. First ionization energy generally rises across a period and falls down a group. There are exceptions. The tendency to give up electrons also helps explain why metallic character generally increases towards the lower left. These are broad trends. Atomic size depends on its definition, and ionization has exceptions. More on those next time. Electron structure helps explain both the table's shape and the properties of its elements. Structure connects to behaviour.
