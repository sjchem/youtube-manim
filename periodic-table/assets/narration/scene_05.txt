Begin with one s, the lowest orbital in hydrogen. One box represents one spatial orbital, with room for two electrons. Hydrogen has one electron. It occupies the one s orbital. We write one s to the one. The superscript counts the electrons in that subshell. Hydrogen takes the first position in our growing table. Helium has two electrons. They occupy the same spatial orbital with opposite spins. Its configuration is one s squared. Both available spin states are occupied. A third electron cannot join this orbital. To build lithium, it must enter a different state: two s, beginning the second shell. That is why the first period contains exactly two elements: hydrogen and helium. The count comes from one s. No third place is available in that subshell. But where should helium sit? By configuration, it is an s element, with one s squared. By chemistry, it belongs with the noble gases. Its only shell is closed, and removing an electron costs a great deal of energy. So we place it on the far right. The capacities explain the widths; chemistry helps organize the columns. Helium reminds us that the drawing is a useful convention. Our first row is complete. Lithium begins the next one, with a fresh outer shell.
