## Description

Squeeze carbon dioxide at room temperature and droplets appear — something
PV = nRT never predicts. In Part 2 of our States of Matter series we follow the
same molecules from an ideal gas to a real liquid: why molecules at one
temperature move at many speeds (the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution, built from
a 3D velocity cloud), which assumptions of kinetic theory fail first, how
attraction and molecular size make real gases deviate, how van der Waals
repaired the gas equation, what the compressibility factor Z measures, and how
Andrews' carbon-dioxide experiments revealed the critical point — where gas and
liquid stop being different. We finish with the chemistry of liquids: vapour
pressure and boiling, surface tension and viscosity.

Covers NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Unit 5 (States of Matter), §5.7–5.11:
Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution and molecular speeds, kinetic molecular theory,
real gases and the van der Waals equation, compressibility factor and Boyle
temperature, liquefaction and critical constants, vapour pressure, surface
tension and viscosity.

The molecular demonstrations are reproducible models: a 2D hard-disk simulation
that starts at one speed, live hard-sphere gases, and the van der Waals model
(with Maxwell's construction) for the isotherms and the P–V–T surface. Every
number on screen is computed from shared physical data.
