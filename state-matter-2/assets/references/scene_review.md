## Scientific corrections that matter

- **Pressure reference:** in the van der Waals model,
  P* = P + a(n/V)² = nRT/(V − nb). P* is not nRT/V when b is nonzero.
- **Finite-size approximation:** b is an excluded-volume parameter; the
  low-density hard-sphere result is b = 4N_A v. V − nb is the model's
  approximation, rather than an exact geometry formula at every density.
- **Critical behaviour:** below Tc, two phases can coexist at the saturation
  pressure. Above both Tc and Pc the fluid is supercritical. The coexistence
  endpoint is not a global maximum of pressure on a P–V–T surface.
- **Attraction and size:** the model relation Tc = 8a/(27Rb) depends on a/b.
  A comparison involving parameters fitted to critical constants is an
  illustration of that relation, not independent experimental proof.
- **Equilibrium and boiling:** saturated vapour pressure needs both phases
  present; sealing alone does not forbid bubbles or boiling. The ordinary
  boiling criterion neglects curvature and depth corrections. Cooker pressure
  is stated as absolute pressure.
- **Interfaces:** one interface has force γ per unit edge length; the two-sided
  soap film shown has F = 2γL. Heating does not switch off molecular attractions.
- **Viscosity:** honey is not a polymer tangle. Molecular crowding, hydrogen
  bonding and rearrangement matter; the polymer is a separate example.

The water saturation curve now uses the continuous IAPWS correlation through
its critical endpoint. The elementary van der Waals and molecular pictures
remain labelled models: they are teaching demonstrations, not quantitative
molecular-dynamics predictions of an actual liquid.
