[warm, conversational delivery] Surface tension is about a liquid's surface. Viscosity is about its flow. [short pause] Trap a liquid between two plates. Hold the bottom plate still, and slide the top one. The liquid touching each plate moves with it. In between, the liquid moves in smooth layers, each a little faster than the one below. This is laminar flow. The speed changes steadily with height. That rate of change, d u by d z, is the velocity gradient. [pause] Neighbouring layers exchange momentum. Molecules wander between them, carrying momentum, while intermolecular forces also transfer it. Together, these processes resist relative sliding. This is not dry friction between solid sheets. So it takes a steady force to keep the top plate moving. For many liquids, the force per unit area is proportional to the velocity gradient. F over A equals eta times d u by d z. [short pause] Eta is the viscosity. It is measured in pascal seconds. Liquids that obey this simple law are called Newtonian.
