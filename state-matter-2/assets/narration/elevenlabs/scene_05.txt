[warm, conversational delivery] Take every molecule's velocity arrow, and move its tail to one common point. The tips form a cloud in velocity space. Every direction is equally likely, so the cloud is round. It is densest at the centre, and thins out toward high velocities. A molecule's speed is the distance of its tip from the centre. So to count the molecules with one particular speed, count the tips inside a thin spherical shell. [short pause] Near the centre, the shell is tiny. It holds very few tips, even though the cloud is densest there. That is why very slow molecules are rare. [short pause] Make the shell bigger. Its area grows as four pi u squared, so it gathers more and more tips. Far out, the cloud fades faster than the shell can grow, and the count falls again. [pause] [emphasized] A growing shell times a fading cloud: that product is the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution of molecular speeds. Written out, it has a u squared factor from the shell, and an exponential factor that makes high kinetic energies rare. [short pause] [warmly] You don't need to memorise it. What matters is the shape.
