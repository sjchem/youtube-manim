[warm, conversational delivery] Start with attraction. Imagine a gas dense enough that neighbouring molecules feel each other's pull. Deep inside the gas, a molecule is tugged from every side. On average, those pulls cancel. [short pause] Now follow one molecule toward the wall. Its neighbours are now mostly behind it. Their combined pull points back into the gas. So it strikes the wall a little more softly. Every molecule arriving at the wall feels the same backward tug, and together they push less. When attraction dominates, a real gas exerts less pressure than an ideal gas with the same amount, volume and temperature. P real is less than P ideal. [short pause] [emphasized] This is called a negative deviation. [pause] The picture of a single tug is a simplification. A full treatment averages over every pair of molecules. But the conclusion survives: attraction lowers the pressure.
