[warm, conversational delivery] Liquid molecules attract one another. So how can any of them escape into the gas? [short pause] Remember the speed distribution. Even in a liquid, energies are spread out. At any moment, some surface molecules can break free, when their energy, direction of motion and local surroundings allow it. The highlighted tail is a simplified picture of that possibility. This is evaporation. Seal the container. Above the liquid, vapour builds up. Some vapour molecules strike the surface and are captured again: condensation. The more vapour there is, the faster it condenses. [pause] Eventually, the two rates become equal. Molecules still leave, and still return, but the amount of vapour stops changing. This is a dynamic equilibrium. The pressure of this vapour is the equilibrium vapour pressure, also called the saturated vapour pressure. It depends on the liquid and its temperature, not on the size of the container, provided liquid and vapour are still present at equilibrium. [pause] Warm the liquid. More molecules have enough energy to escape, the balance shifts, and the vapour pressure rises. For otherwise similar liquids, stronger cohesion tends to hold molecules more tightly and lower the vapour pressure at the same temperature.
