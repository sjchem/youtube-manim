[warm, conversational delivery] We began with an ideal gas: a crowd of tiny points, described by one elegant equation. [short pause] But real molecules are richer than that. They move at many different speeds. They attract one another. And they take up space. When molecules are far apart and fast, those details fade, and the ideal gas equation works beautifully. When they are squeezed together or slowed down, those same details take control. [pause] They explain why real gases deviate from the ideal equation. Why cooling and compression can make a liquid. And why, at the critical point, gas and liquid become one. They explain why liquids evaporate, why drops pull themselves round, and why some liquids flow while others crawl. [pause] [warmly] The molecules never turned into something else. What changed was how fast they move, how closely they are packed, and how strongly they interact. [long pause] [warmly] That is the chemistry of gases and liquids.
