[warm, conversational delivery] In the eighteen sixties, Thomas Andrews squeezed carbon dioxide at constant temperature, and measured its pressure as its volume shrank. Follow one isotherm, at twenty-one point five degrees Celsius. At first, the gas behaves much like any gas. Squeeze it, and the pressure rises. [short pause] [curious] Then, at a certain volume, the first drops of liquid appear. [short pause] Keep squeezing. The pressure stops rising. More and more gas condenses, while the pressure stays constant. Liquid and vapour now coexist, in equilibrium. [pause] Why doesn't the pressure rise? Squeezing no longer crowds the vapour. It simply turns more of it into liquid. Finally, the last bubble of gas disappears. Now we are compressing a liquid, and liquids barely compress. The pressure shoots up. [pause] [emphasized] One substance, one temperature, three different behaviours. Gas and liquid are not different substances. They are two states of the same molecules.
