[warm, conversational delivery] In our last lesson, we discovered something remarkable. Countless molecules, moving at random inside a container, obey one simple equation. P V equals n R T. Pressure, volume, temperature and amount, all tied together in a single line. [pause] [emphasized] But there is a problem. Take carbon dioxide at room temperature, and squeeze it. The pressure climbs, just as the equation predicts. [short pause] Keep squeezing, and something happens that the equation never mentioned. [short pause] [curious] Droplets appear. A liquid gathers on the floor of the container. In the ideal gas model, molecules have no size and feel no attraction. Particles like that could never cling together into a liquid. [pause] So the equation is not the whole truth. It is an approximation, and a very good one, under the right conditions. To see where it breaks, we have to look at what happens between molecules, not only when they strike the walls. [pause] [warmly] This is Beyond Ideal Gases: how molecules become liquids.
