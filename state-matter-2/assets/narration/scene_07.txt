Now heat the nitrogen, from three hundred to six hundred kelvin. The whole curve shifts to higher speeds, and spreads out. Its peak drops, because the area under the curve is the total fraction of molecules, and that is always one. For an ideal gas, the average translational kinetic energy is three halves k B T. So doubling the absolute temperature doubles the average kinetic energy. But the typical speeds rise only by the square root of two: about one point four one times. Speed grows as the square root of temperature. Mass matters too. Here are nitrogen and chlorine at the same temperature. On average, their molecules carry the same translational kinetic energy. Chlorine molecules are about two and a half times heavier, so they move more slowly. Their curve is narrower, and peaks further to the left. Typical speeds go as one over the square root of molar mass. Light molecules are fast. Heavy molecules are slow. So the speed distribution depends on both temperature and mass.
