Now put both corrections into the ideal gas equation. Replace the pressure with P plus a n squared over V squared. Replace the volume with V minus n b. The result is the van der Waals equation. P plus a n squared over V squared, times V minus n b, equals n R T. Let's test it. One mole of carbon dioxide in a one-litre flask, at three hundred kelvin. The ideal gas equation predicts about twenty-four point nine bar. Correct for size first. With less free room, the pressure rises to about twenty-six point one bar. Now correct for attraction. That takes away about three point six bar. The van der Waals equation predicts about twenty-two point four bar: about ten percent below the ideal value. Now give the same mole a hundred litres. Both corrections almost vanish, and the van der Waals equation becomes the ideal gas equation. The ideal gas equation is a useful approximation. Van der Waals extends it with two physical ideas: molecules attract, and molecules take up space.
