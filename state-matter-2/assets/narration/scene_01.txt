In our last lesson, we discovered something remarkable. Countless molecules, moving at random inside a container, obey one simple equation. P V equals n R T. Pressure, volume, temperature and amount, all tied together in a single line. But there is a problem. Take carbon dioxide at room temperature, and squeeze it. The pressure climbs, just as the equation predicts. Keep squeezing, and something happens that the equation never mentioned. Droplets appear. A liquid gathers on the floor of the container. In the ideal gas model, molecules have no size and feel no attraction. Particles like that could never cling together into a liquid. So the equation is not the whole truth. It is an approximation, and a very good one, under the right conditions. To see where it breaks, we have to look at what happens between molecules, not only when they strike the walls. This is Beyond Ideal Gases: how molecules become liquids.
