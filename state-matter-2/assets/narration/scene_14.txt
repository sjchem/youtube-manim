The second correction is for size. Ideal molecules are points, free to roam the entire volume. Real molecules are not. Picture two molecules as hard spheres, each with diameter d. They can touch, but they can't overlap. So the centre of the second molecule can never come closer than d to the centre of the first. Around each molecule there is a forbidden sphere, with radius d, not d over two. Its volume is eight times the volume of the molecule itself. That forbidden sphere belongs to a pair of molecules, so share it between the two: four times the molecule's own volume, for each molecule. For a mole of molecules, that excluded-volume parameter is the constant b. Van der Waals approximates the volume available for motion as V minus n b. Notice what this means. b is not simply the volume of the molecules. For hard spheres, it is about four times larger, because what matters is where the centres can go.
