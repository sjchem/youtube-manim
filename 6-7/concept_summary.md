# Scientific Concept Summary

**Video title:** Why 6 Is Perfect and 7 Is Mysterious

This animation contrasts two consecutive integers that feel mathematically opposite.

**6 is visible order.** It is the first perfect number because its proper divisors add back to itself:

```text
1 + 2 + 3 = 6
```

It also carries a rare multiplicative symmetry:

```text
1 x 2 x 3 = 6
```

In geometry, 6 becomes the regular hexagon. Each interior angle is 120 degrees, so three hexagons meet exactly around a point:

```text
120 + 120 + 120 = 360
```

That is why hexagons tile the plane without gaps or overlaps, alongside equilateral triangles and squares.

**7 is hidden order.** It is prime, cannot be split into smaller multiplicative parts, and it resists some simple geometric patterns. A regular heptagon is not constructible using only an ideal compass and straightedge, and its interior angle does not fit evenly around a point for regular tessellation.

But 7 is not chaos. Its reciprocal reveals a six-step cycle:

```text
1/7 = 0.142857142857...
```

The digits `142857` rotate when multiplied by 1 through 6:

```text
142857 x 1 = 142857
142857 x 2 = 285714
142857 x 3 = 428571
142857 x 4 = 571428
142857 x 5 = 714285
142857 x 6 = 857142
```

The story is not "6 is good and 7 is weird." The deeper point is that mathematics contains both visible order and hidden order.
