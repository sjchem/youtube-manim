Shape has one more surprise. Together with bond polarity, it determines whether a molecule has an overall dipole. Start with hydrogen chloride. Chlorine pulls the shared electrons harder than hydrogen does. So the electron cloud leans toward chlorine. Chlorine ends up slightly negative, hydrogen slightly positive. We can draw that imbalance as an arrow, a bond dipole, pointing toward the atom that pulls harder. Because it's an arrow, it has a size and a direction. It's a vector. And vectors add. Now carbon dioxide. Oxygen pulls harder than carbon, so both bonds are polar. Two arrows, pointing outward. Add them, tip to tail. They are equal and exactly opposite. They cancel. Carbon dioxide has polar bonds, but no overall dipole. A nonpolar molecule. Now water. Oxygen pulls on both bonds again. Two arrows, both pointing toward oxygen. But water is bent. Add these two arrows, tip to tail, and they don't cancel. The sum points toward the oxygen side. Water is a polar molecule. Both have polar bonds, but their different shapes make the arrows add differently.
