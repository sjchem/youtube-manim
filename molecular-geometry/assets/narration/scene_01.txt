Look at these two molecules. Carbon dioxide has three atoms. Water has three atoms too. We can draw both as straight lines on paper. But a Lewis drawing need not show the real shape. But lift them off the page, into three dimensions, and something changes. Carbon dioxide stays perfectly straight. One hundred and eighty degrees. Water bends, to an angle of about one hundred and four point five degrees. Why? Why don't water's atoms simply line up? These shapes are not random. The answer is hidden in electrons we cannot see. This is molecular geometry, explained visually.
