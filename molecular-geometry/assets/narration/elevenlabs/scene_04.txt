[warm, conversational delivery] So the real skill is counting regions around the central atom. One counting rule makes it easy. A single bond counts as one region. A double bond also counts as one. Its two shared pairs point the same way, between the same two atoms. [curious] A triple bond? Still one region. And every lone pair on the central atom counts as one more region. [warmly] Chemists write this as a short code. A is the central atom. Each X is a bonded atom. Each E is a lone pair. Ammonia has three bonded hydrogens and one lone pair on nitrogen. A X three E. Add them up, three plus one, and you get four regions. That total is called the steric number. [short pause] [thoughtful] And the steric number picks the arrangement.
