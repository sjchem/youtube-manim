[warm, conversational delivery] Picture a central atom, surrounded by regions of electron density. A bond is one region. A lone pair is another. Each region is rich in electrons, so in this simple picture, the regions repel one another. Push two of them together, and watch. [thoughtful] They spread apart until they sit on opposite sides, straight through the atom. One hundred and eighty degrees. Add a third region. The three push each other around until they share the circle equally. One hundred and twenty degrees apart, all in one flat plane. Now a fourth. Lay the triangle flat, and drop the new region on top. The three below fold downward, like an umbrella closing, until all four are equally spaced in three dimensions. [short pause] [warmly] A tetrahedron. In this simplified model, the repulsion found the arrangement on its own. Here a simple computer model does the pushing: points on a sphere that repel, settling where the total repulsion is lowest. [thoughtful] This idea has a name: valence shell electron pair repulsion. VSEPR, for short. Two regions, linear. Three, trigonal planar. Four, tetrahedral. Count the regions, and the arrangement follows.
