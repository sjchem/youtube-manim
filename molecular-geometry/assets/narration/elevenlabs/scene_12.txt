Xenon tetrafluoride. Four bonds, two lone pairs. A X four E two. Now hide them. Four fluorines in a perfect square, with the xenon at the centre. Completely flat. Square planar. [short pause] [curious] How can a molecule with six electron regions look like a flat sheet? [warmly] Because two of its regions are lone pairs, one above the plane and one below, pressing equally from both sides. Octahedral, square pyramidal, square planar. With that, we have the main families of VSEPR shapes, from two regions to six.
