Now methane. Carbon has four bonds and no lone pairs. Four regions. A X four. The flat guess puts them in a square, ninety degrees apart. But in a square, every region has two neighbours crowding it at ninety degrees. There is room to do better. Let the regions use the third dimension. Two tilt up, two tilt down, and every gap opens wider. Now every region is the same distance from every other. That is the tetrahedron. Put hydrogens at the four tips, and you have methane. Every angle between two bonds is the same. Where does that number come from? Place the carbon at the centre of a cube. The four hydrogens sit on alternate corners of the cube, as far apart as they can get. Two bond directions are then one, one, one, and one, minus one, minus one. Their dot product is minus one, and each has length root three. So the cosine of the angle between them is minus one third. Take the inverse cosine, and out comes one hundred and nine point four seven degrees. Not a number to memorise. Just the geometry of four equal directions.
