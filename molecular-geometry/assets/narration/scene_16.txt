For ammonia and water, introductory courses usually say S P three as well, with lone pairs sitting in hybrid orbitals. It's a useful picture. But it isn't the whole story. Perfect S P three hybrids would give one hundred and nine point five degrees. The real angles are smaller. The labels don't produce the measured angles. For water especially, the actual orbitals are more nuanced than four identical hybrids. So keep the two models in their own lanes. VSEPR predicts the approximate geometry, by counting bonds and lone pairs. Hybridization describes it, with directional orbitals for sigma bonds, and leftover p orbitals for pi bonds. You may also meet labels like S P three D for five regions, and S P three D two for six. Treat them as historical textbook labels. Modern calculations show d orbitals play only a minor part here.
