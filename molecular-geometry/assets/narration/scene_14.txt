Now mix one s with two p orbitals. Out come three S P two hybrids. They lie in one plane, one hundred and twenty degrees apart. The same directions VSEPR gave three regions. One p orbital is left out of the mix. It stands straight up through the plane. Boron in boron trifluoride is often described this way: three S P two hybrids, each overlapping with a fluorine. Now mix the s with all three p orbitals. Four S P three hybrids. They point to the corners of a tetrahedron. Nothing is left over. Methane's four bonds line up with them perfectly. But here is the beautiful part. Those angles were not put in by hand. In this ideal model, the equivalent hybrids are chosen to be orthogonal. That means their wave functions have zero overlap. With the same s character in each equivalent hybrid, orthogonality fixes their directions. The cosine of their angle is minus one over n. S P: minus one. One hundred and eighty degrees. S P two: minus a half. One hundred and twenty. S P three: minus a third. One hundred and nine point five. The very same minus one third we found with the cube. Two completely different routes, repulsion and orbital mixing, land on the same directions. That is why the two pictures fit together so well.
