Let's put the rule to work, starting with carbon dioxide. Carbon makes two double bonds, one to each oxygen, and keeps no lone pairs. Two regions. Two regions push to opposite sides, so the oxygens line up across the carbon. A X two. Linear. Spin it any way you like. It stays a straight rod. Notice that each double bond holds four electrons, yet still acts as a single region. What matters is direction, not how many pairs share it. Next, boron trifluoride. Boron makes three bonds to fluorine and keeps no lone pairs. Three regions. Three regions spread around a circle. Every fluorine sits one hundred and twenty degrees from the next. Tip it on its edge, and the whole molecule vanishes into a line. All four atoms lie in one plane. A X three. Trigonal planar. Flat, like a three pointed star. Two regions make a line. Three make a flat triangle. But four regions cannot stay flat.
