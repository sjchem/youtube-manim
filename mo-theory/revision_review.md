# Molecular Orbital Theory — visual and narration review

Reviewed on 24 October 2026 against the supplied brief and the preceding voice-pacing discussion. All **23 chapters** remain independently renderable; the planned film remains **34:10**.

## Main changes

- Reduced particle/title and wave/caption conflicts, gave wide p-orbital views more room, and shortened the mixing arrow that crossed the lower caption.
- Kept phase colours consistent in every orbital orientation. Nitrogen now highlights one orbital contribution at a time rather than presenting all clouds at full strength.
- Added shaded side/top faces and copper reflections to the electromagnets, with polished spheres for the small helium and oxygen illustrations.
- Replaced the hydrogen-like closing cloud with oxygen’s two rotating pi-star views, each with one spin marker.
- Corrected the hydrogen-ion dissociation reference, qualified the node/force explanation, and clarified symmetry, helium binding, magnetic alignment and representative ion distances.

## Narration and pauses

The revised script has **4,684 spoken words** and **252 timed beats**. Most ordinary explanations use about **140–150 words per finished minute**; dense orbital demonstrations, the oxygen reveal, the challenge and the closing card use less. Across the whole film the rate is about **137 words per finished minute**.

At the measured cloned-voice pace of 100 words in 35 seconds, estimated speech occupies **27:19**, leaving about **6:51** distributed through the edit for viewing, movement, breathing and the closing fade. Every individual beat fits below the 2.85 words/second ceiling. The timings are estimates, not guarantees about generated audio.

Clean and tagged full-scene scripts, all numbered beat files, Markdown cues and the JSON cue sheet have been regenerated. The JSON now includes scene word counts, estimated speech and pause totals, and a suggested pause start for every beat. Editing instructions stay outside the spoken text.

Generate or record the revised text, measure each clip, and place the numbered clips at their cues. Hold the current visual through the remaining interval rather than shortening the whole video to an uninterrupted voice file. Pause tags guide performance and cannot promise exact silence durations; ElevenLabs v4 does not accept SSML break timing. See [ElevenLabs pause guidance](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/core-capabilities/text-to-speech/how-can-i-add-pauses).
