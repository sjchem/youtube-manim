Start with something simpler than an orbital: a wave. Think about its shape first. Electrons in atoms behave like standing waves. They have peaks, troughs, and a sign, plus or minus. Put two waves together with their peaks lined up, and they reinforce. The sum is taller than either one. We add their amplitudes point by point. Now shift one by half a wavelength, so that every peak meets a trough. They cancel. The cancellation depends on their relative phase. Same two waves. Different phase. Completely different result. No electron has vanished in this analogy. Now swap the waves for two hydrogen orbitals. Along the line through both nuclei, each one is a sharp peak that fades with distance. This curve is a slice through space. Its height is the wave function, psi. Its sign is its phase. Probability comes from its squared magnitude. Add the two in phase, and the waves pile up in the middle. Subtract them, and they cancel exactly halfway, leaving a point where the wave is zero. A node. Remember those two pictures: piling up, and cancelling. They are the whole story, in miniature.
