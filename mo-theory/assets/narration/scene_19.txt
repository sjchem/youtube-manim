Add one electron to oxygen. Where can it go? The lowest open spot is in pi star, pairing with one of the unpaired electrons. That's superoxide, O two minus. Antibonding electrons: five. Bond order: eight minus five, over two. One and a half. The total charge is negative. Add one more, and pi star is completely full. Peroxide, O two two minus. Bond order one. Each electron we add goes into an antibonding orbital, so each one weakens the bond. And the bond stretches as the bond order falls. The ion lengths shown are representative values in salts; their exact distances depend on their chemical surroundings. Magnetism changes too. One unpaired electron, then two, then one, then none. Peroxide, with every spin paired, is diamagnetic. We count the unpaired spins for each species separately. Four species, one diagram. It predicts each bond order and each magnetic behaviour. Add an electron, and the bond can weaken. Remove one, and it can strengthen. It all depends on where the electron goes. The same counting works in both directions. Occupancy is the decisive information.
