Nitrogen. Each atom brings five valence electrons. Five plus five: ten. Core electrons are already set aside. Nitrogen uses the first ordering, with the pi pair below sigma two p. Fill from the bottom. Two electrons into sigma two s. Two into sigma star two s. So far, they cancel. Both bonding and antibonding are occupied. Now the pi pair. One electron in each, spins parallel, as Hund's rule says. Then the next two pair up. Four electrons in the two pi orbitals. Each orbital now contains opposite spins. That's eight. The last two go into sigma two p. The bonding sigma level can hold them. Ten electrons, every one of them placed. No electron has been created or lost. The configuration reads like this: sigma two s squared, sigma star two s squared, pi two p to the fourth, sigma two p squared. Every orbital that's occupied holds a pair. No electron is left alone. Pairing determines the magnetic prediction too. Count the bonding electrons. Two, plus four, plus two: eight. Antibonding: two. Include the lower bonding pair in the total. Eight minus two, over two. Bond order three. The occupied antibonding pair subtracts one bond. A triple bond, exactly what the Lewis structure draws. N, triple bond, N. Two very different pictures, one answer. The orbital explanation also tells us about magnetism.
