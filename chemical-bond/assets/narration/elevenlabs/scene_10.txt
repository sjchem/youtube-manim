[warm, conversational delivery] Sodium and chlorine. Sodium has one valence electron, sitting alone in a new shell. Chlorine has seven. The familiar story says sodium hands its electron to chlorine, and both end up with eight. Simple. [curious] But let's check the energy. Pulling the electron off a sodium atom in the gas costs four hundred and ninety-six kilojoules per mole. Giving that electron to a chlorine atom releases three hundred and forty-nine. Add them up. Transferring the electron, all by itself, costs about one hundred and forty-seven. [thoughtful] It is uphill. So electron transfer alone does not explain salt. Something else must pay the bill. [warmly] Here is the something else. We now have a positive ion and a negative ion. Opposite charges, attracting. Let them come together, and the energy drops, by far more than the transfer cost. Remember the principle: what matters is the energy of the whole system, not one step of it. And in real salt, an ion is not attracted by just one partner. It is surrounded. It is time to build a crystal, one ion at a time.
