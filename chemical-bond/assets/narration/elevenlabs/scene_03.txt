[warm, conversational delivery] Two protons, two electrons. The moving dots are position samples, not little planets following paths. Every particle feels every other one. Opposite charges attract, so each electron is pulled by both nuclei. Like charges repel. Nucleus pushes on nucleus, and electron pushes on electron. Far apart, none of this matters much. Each atom simply keeps its own electron. [curious] Closer in, something interesting happens. Each electron can now spend time near both nuclei, so it feels both pulls at once. Electron density gathers between the nuclei, and that shared cloud attracts both of them, drawing them together. But push them closer still, and the two nuclei repel more and more strongly. The push starts to win. [thoughtful] Quantum mechanics adds another ingredient. Confining an electron more tightly generally raises its kinetic energy; a classical force picture is not enough. [warmly] Tracking every force at once is hopeless. So let's track one number instead: the total energy of the whole system.
