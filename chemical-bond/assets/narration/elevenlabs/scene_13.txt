[warm, conversational delivery] Copper presents a different problem: many closely packed atoms, with electrons in quantum states extending through the solid. Each copper atom has twelve nearest neighbours. A set of isolated two-atom electron pairs is not an adequate picture. The important valence electrons occupy delocalised states throughout the crystal. The lattice contains positive ion cores and mobile electronic charge. [thoughtful] Delocalised means the electrons belong to the extended solid. This is often called the electron sea. It is a simplified picture of electrons spread through the entire solid. The sea attracts every core, and every core attracts the sea. That shared attraction is the metallic bond. Apply a voltage. Electrons acquire a small drift toward the positive side; conventional current points the other way. [curious] Now hit the metal. A layer of cores slides over the next, but the sea simply flows along. The metal bends instead of breaking. Try the same with salt. Shift one layer, and suddenly positive faces positive, and negative faces negative. The repulsion splits the crystal. That is why salt shatters, and copper bends. [warmly] Shared, transferred, or delocalised: three different ways for electrons to lower the energy of the whole.
