Imagine two hydrogen atoms, drifting in the dark. Each one is a tiny positive nucleus, wrapped in a cloud made by a single electron. Bring them closer. The two nuclei repel each other. The two electrons repel each other, too. And yet the atoms don't fly apart. They settle together, at one particular distance. Why don't they drift away again? Why don't they collapse into each other? What is holding them together? The same question hides inside a grain of salt, and inside a block of copper. Three very different ways of sticking together. Yet all of them obey the same physics. This is the story of the chemical bond.
