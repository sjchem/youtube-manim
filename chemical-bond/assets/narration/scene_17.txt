We now understand why atoms stick together. But here is something strange. Carbon dioxide: one carbon atom, two oxygen atoms, in a perfectly straight line. Water: one oxygen, two hydrogens. And it is bent, at about one hundred and four and a half degrees. Both are made of three atoms joined by bonds. So why is one straight, and the other bent? Why don't bonded atoms simply line up? What decides the angles between bonds? To answer that, we have to leave the single bond behind, and step into the three-dimensional world of molecular geometry.
