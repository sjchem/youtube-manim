# Animation plan — Chemical Bonding, Explained Visually

18 chapters, 28:10 (28:00 of story plus a 10-second subscribe card). Four
chapters use real 3D (`ThreeDScene` with lit, depth-sorted surfaces): 04, 08,
09, 17. Every other chapter is flat, but its atoms are seeded 3D point clouds
and its crystals are hand-projected 3D lattices that turn, so nothing on screen
is static.

**One visual grammar** (defined once in `manim_scenes/common.py`):

| Idea | Picture | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | a glowing warm ball (not to scale) | orange |
| Electron density | a turning cloud of seeded dots; a forming bond is the same dots flowing into one cloud | cyan |
| Attraction / repulsion | live lines between particles | teal / red (dashed) |
| Energy | the E(r) curve, its marker, energy numbers | gold |
| Energy released / required | downward / upward arrows | teal / orange |
| Ions; δ+ and δ− | shaded balls; partial-charge labels | purple (+), green (−) |
| σ overlap / π overlap | LCAO isosurfaces | blue / purple (second π of N₂: rose) |
| Metal | copper cores in a drifting electron sea | copper + cyan |
| A confirmed result | check marks, octet rings | green |

## Reusable components (in `common.py`)

`PairCloud` (two atoms → one shared cloud, with lean), `BondCloud` (leaning
polar cloud), `ElectronCloud`/`Atom`, `bind_pair` + `pair_nuclei` (a molecule
riding a separation tracker), `force_line` (live attraction/repulsion lines that
fade with distance), `EnergyPlot` (Morse curve, marker, depth arrow, model
terms), `LewisAtom` + `pair_targets` + `bond_lines` + `octet_ring`,
`ProjectedCrystal` (assembly, highlights, live links), `MetalSea` (drifting
electrons, slippable layers), `ion_layers`, `GridSurface`/`Isosurface`
(computed meshes that rebuild while morphing), orbital grids
(`sigma_s_grid`, `sigma_p_grid`, `pi_grid`, `single_p_grid`), the 3D energy
well (`well_grid`), smooth vector sphere shading (`polished_ball`), and ball-and-stick helpers (`atom_ball`, `bond_rod`).

Opening labels clear after a few seconds. Captions use short phrases, and overlapping caption replacements use separate fade phases. The 3D geometry teaser uses one shared pm-to-screen scale for CO₂ and H₂O.
