## Description

How can one glass of sugar water be 0.524 M, 0.556 m, 0.00990 and 9.09 % — all at
the same time, and all correct?

In this visual journey through solution concentration, we keep returning to one
solution — 18.0 g of glucose dissolved in 180.0 g of water — and change only
what we compare the glucose with. Molarity divides by the volume of the whole
solution. Molality divides by the mass of the solvent. Mole fraction divides by
every molecule present. Percentages, ppm and ppb each need their own little
letters. Along the way: why heating a sealed solution changes its molarity but
not its molality, why the same sulfuric acid can be 0.500 N or 1.00 N, how
density converts molarity into molality, and why dilution is just
conservation.

No memorising. Just one question: what are we actually comparing?

Numbers use rounded molar masses (glucose 180 g/mol, water 18 g/mol) and an
illustrative measured volume of 191.0 mL. The heating example assumes the
solution expands like water between 20 and 70 °C. Particle pictures are
illustrations, not to scale.

Sources: IUPAC Gold Book and Orange Book; D. Harvey, Analytical Chemistry 2.1
(LibreTexts); CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics; OpenStax Chemistry 2e.
