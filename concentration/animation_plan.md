# Animation plan — Concentration, Explained Visually

21 scenes, 35:00, grouped into the brief's 12 YouTube chapters. Visual
language everywhere: **solute gold, solvent blue, solution volume purple,
solvent mass cyan, solution mass rose, counted things teal, results green,
traps red**. Four recurring moves: **isolate** (light what is counted),
**measure** (draw the denominator), **transform** (picture → fraction),
**test** (change something physical and watch the numbers).

## Act I — the measurement problem (00:00–12:00)

**01 The mystery (00:00, 105 s, 3D).** Darkness; a turning beaker of 600 water
dots. A crystal of six glowing glucose beads falls in and dissolves (true
1 : 100 molecule ratio). The camera dives among the molecules, pulls back, and
four numbers appear around the glass on glowing threads: 0.524 M, 0.556 m,
0.00990, 9.09 %. "ONE SOLUTION. FOUR CORRECT ANSWERS." Green ticks; each label
becomes "glucose / ?" with a coloured question-mark denominator; the glass
glows purple, then its water cyan — nothing in it changes. "memorise" is
struck out for "picture"; title card over the zoomed solution.

**02 Counting in moles (01:45, 70 s, 3D molecules).** Computed glucose chair and
water turn on either side. A digital balance weighs 18.0 g + 180.0 g =
198.0 g (mass adds). A bundle of beads is "a mole". n = mass / molar mass gives
0.100 mol and 10.0 mol. Bars: ×10 the mass, a grid of 100 dots vs one gold
bead: ×100 the molecules. Two teal counters stay.

**03 Mass adds, volume doesn't (02:55, 65 s, 3D).** Two graduated cylinders of
50 mL ethanol and water pour into a third: 96.5 mL, the 100 mL mark glows.
A labelled packing picture shows small spheres nestling into gaps. The glucose
beaker returns outlined in purple: 191.0 mL, "measured, not added". Four
quantity chips; "concentration = one of these / another".

**04 Molarity (04:00, 130 s, 3D).** Water fades, glucose glows: n = 0.100 mol
(numerator). Purple outline around the whole liquid: V = 0.1910 L
(denominator). c = n_solute / V_solution, molarity / amount concentration,
mol/L = M; numbers fly into the fraction: 0.524 M boxed. Oblique cubes: 1 L
holds 0.524 mol, 100 mL holds 0.0524 mol — same ratio. "solution ✓ solvent ✗";
c ≠ n / V_water struck out in red. A volumetric flask appears with a "?".

**05 Filling to the mark (06:10, 125 s, 3D).** Goal 1.000 L of 0.500 M: 0.500 mol =
90.0 g. Tempting way: glucose into 1 L of water — the liquid rises past the
mark (computed), "< 0.500 M", red cross. Right way: glucose into the flask,
some water, swirl (the model spins), fill slowly through the thin neck until
the meniscus meets the glowing line, stopper, invert (rocking). 0.500 mol /
1.000 L = 0.500 M boxed. Catch: "calibrated at 20 °C"; a thermometer climbs
to 40 °C and the liquid creeps past the line.

**06 Molality (08:15, 120 s, 3D).** Purple volume glow on, then off; only the
solvent lights cyan. Water beads drop onto a balance: 180.0 g = 0.1800 kg.
m = n_solute / m_solvent. The key transformation: c = 0.100 mol / 0.1910 L —
numerator framed "stays" — the denominator morphs into 0.1800 kg as the tag
changes from "solution volume" to "solvent mass"; = 0.556 mol/kg, 0.556 m
boxed. Side by side c and m, glass toggling its glows. Thermometer swings
80 → 5 °C while the balance stays at 180.0 g; then the glass warms and its
volume swells: "volume?".

**07 Where molality matters (10:15, 105 s).** Two 2D molecule panels (a
picture): pure water locks into a lattice at 0 °C on a vertical temperature
scale; the glucose solution stays liquid until about −1.03 °C. ΔT_f = K_f m,
K_f ≈ 1.86, 1.86 × 0.556 ≈ 1.03 °C; ΔT_b ≈ 0.28 °C. One gold bead beside 100
blue: a ratio of particles; 1 kg water ≈ 55.6 mol, a fixed number of
molecules; "fixed ratio while liquid composition is conserved". Question card.

## Act II — three surprising discoveries (12:00–22:45)

**08 The temperature experiment (12:00, 120 s, 3D — reveal 2).** A closed,
graduated cylinder with expansion headspace; a magnified inset of its 187–199 mL scale; two live
meters (purple molarity, cyan molality) that read the cylinder's own volume.
A heater glows, the thermometer climbs to 70 °C, the molecules speed up and
spread, the inset meniscus rises 191 → 195 mL: molarity ticks down to 0.513
(red arrow, fraction shown), molality holds 0.556 (green tick, fraction
shown). Captions: "molarity ← volume ← temperature", "molality ← masses:
unchanged". Cool down: it climbs back to 0.524.

**09 Why chemists need both (14:00, 90 s).** Two panels: a pipette and bench
chips for molarity; a thermometer and physical-chemistry chips for
molality. A plot of molarity vs molality for glucose (simple model,
labelled): nearly equal when dilute (1 L water ≈ 1 kg), riders slide along the
two curves as a red gap opens. "1 M ≠ 1 m", "later: density connects them".

**10 Normality (15:30, 120 s, 3D).** A computed, turning H₂SO₄ (labelled S, O,
H) with its two acidic hydrogens glowing teal; "1 mol H⁺ = 1 eq".
A quiet caption identifies the structure as bookkeeping: aqueous acid is ionised. N =
equivalents / V_solution, N = M × z; "z = ?", the tempting "2 ?", "the
reaction decides", "first, name the reaction". Historical-unit card and the
IUPAC advice. Two arrows split toward "one proton" and "two protons".

**11 One acid, two normalities (17:30, 120 s, 3D — reveal 3).** Two stages of
the same acid under a fixed "acid stock 0.500 M" label; the label refers to the
original stock before adding base, not to either final mixture. Left: one OH⁻ (with Na⁺);
a proton travels to it and becomes water, the other "stays"; equation;
z = 1 ⇒ N = 0.500. Right: two OH⁻, both protons travel; z = 2 ⇒ N = 1.00.
Purple rays from the unmoved molarity label to both sides. Two rows: molarity
= moles per litre, normality = equivalents per litre … for which reaction?
"1 N H₂SO₄" on a bottle. A 3D crowd of molecules fades in.

**12 Mole fraction (19:30, 110 s, 3D).** A turning cube-shaped crowd of exactly
one glucose and 100 waters. "P(glucose) = ?"; litres and kilograms struck
out; 0.100 : 10.0 → 1 : 100; "101 molecules". A ring picks three random
molecules (seeded): water, water, water; a gold ring finds the glucose:
"101 independent picks" (sampling with replacement, on average). x_i = n_i / Σ n_j, substitution, 0.00990 boxed; a gold
brace "the total, glucose included"; mol/mol cancels; "fixed composition: x stays fixed" while the crowd heats up.

**13 Fractions add to one (21:20, 85 s).** A population bar (blue with a gold
sliver of exactly 0.99 % width); x_water = 10.0 / 10.1 = 0.99010; 0.00990 +
0.99010 = 1 turns green. Two cylindrical liquid cutaways, with elliptical rims and surface reflections,
with molecules escaping;
Raoult's law; P/P° = 1.000 vs 0.990, "about 1 % lower". A large glowing "%".

## Act III — the hidden connections (22:45–31:15)

**14 Three ways to say percent (22:45, 105 s).** A "10 %" bottle with a "?".
% (w/w) = m_solute / m_solution × 100 on a rose balance: 198.0 g → 9.09 %
boxed, "glucose included". % (w/v) with the purple-outlined glass: 18.0 g /
191.0 mL × 100 = 9.42 %. "9.09 % (w/w) ≠ 9.42 % (w/v)". A 5 % (w/v) drip bag =
5 g per 100 mL. Three big chips: w/w, w/v, v/v.

**15 Volume percent and grams per litre (24:30, 90 s, 3D).** A 100 mL flask:
30 mL ethanol (gold beads), then water to the glowing mark — not "+ 70 mL";
"30 + 70 ≠ 100 mL"; 30 % (v/v) "measured at 20 °C"; ABV chip. γ = m / V:
18.0 g / 0.1910 L ≈ 94.2 g/L; g/L → mol/L by ÷ M; γ / M_glucose = c ≈
0.524 M boxed.

**16 One part per million (26:00, 130 s, 3D).** A live-rendered 1000 × 1000
field: one gold dot, zoom out to 1 in 1 000, then to the whole million (an
even blue haze: "somewhere in here…"), zoom back in to the gold dot: 1 ppm =
10⁻⁶. The square shrinks into a turning cube of a thousand such squares
with one gold glint: 1 ppb = 10⁻⁹. 1 ppm ≈ 1 s in 11.5 days; 1 ppb ≈ 1 s in 32
years. Bases: 1 ppm (mass) = 1 mg/kg, 1 ppb = 1 µg/kg, 1 mg/L ≈ 1 ppm in dilute
water — "an approximation, not a definition"; CO₂ > 400 ppm by molecules.

**17 Density connects them (28:10, 95 s, 3D).** Molarity and molality cards
joined by a rose arrow: density. ρ = 198.0 g / 191.0 mL ≈ 1.037 g/mL. A
turning one-litre cube full of solution beside a rose bar of its ≈ 1036.6 g;
glucose glows: 0.524 mol × 180 ≈ 94.2 g (gold slice); water lights: 1036.6 − 94.2
≈ 942.4 g (cyan slice); 0.524 / 0.9424 = 0.556 m boxed. m = c / (ρ − c M_s), read
part by part against the bar.

**18 Dilution (29:45, 90 s, 3D).** 100 mL of 2.00 M stock in a small flask
(20 gold beads: a picture, one per 0.0100 mol). The same beads arc over into a
500 mL flask, which is filled to its mark; "still 20 particles: none vanish,
none appear". 0.200 mol / 0.500 L = 0.400 M; n_before = n_after, n = M V,
M₁V₁ = M₂V₂; 2.00 × 100 / 500 = 0.400 M boxed; "conservation"; "5 × the
volume → ⅕ the concentration".

## Act IV — can you understand it? (31:15–35:00)

**19 Five traps (31:15, 155 s).** Each trap: a red "TRAP k OF 5" chip, the
statement, a 3-2-1 countdown ring, then a visual correction while the
statement shrinks to the top with a red cross: (1) 1 mol into 1 L of water
overflows the litre mark vs a flask filled to the mark; (2) two fractions
with different denominators, 0.524 M vs 0.556 m; (3) take one H⁺: N = M, take
both: N = 2M; (4) mole ratio 0.0100 (red) vs mole fraction 0.00990 (green);
(5) 1 mg/L ≈ 1 ppm in water but ≈ 1.27 ppm in ethanol (1 L ≈ 0.789 kg). Then
"what's on top? / what's on the bottom?" and a glowing hub-and-spoke map.

**20 The concentration map (33:50, 60 s, 3D).** The opening glass returns.
Six nodes wired to it in their colours (molarity, molality, mole fraction,
mass percent, normality, ppm/ppb), lit as named; a rose "density" link
between molarity and molality, "= c × z" by normality. The map dims:
"What are we actually comparing?" The camera dives into the solution and
pulls back under the title CONCENTRATION — Explained Visually.

**21 Subscribe (34:50, 10 s).** House subscribe card between two turning
glasses — one glowing purple (per litre of solution), one with its solvent
lit (per kilogram of solvent).
