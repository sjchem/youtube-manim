Our closed cylinder keeps all the material inside. Headspace above the liquid lets it expand. At room temperature, the liquid reads one hundred and ninety-one millilitres. Two meters. Molarity: zero point five two four. Molality: zero point five five six. Now warm it gently, to about seventy degrees Celsius. Watch the molecules. They move faster, and push each other a little further apart. The liquid expands. If it expands about as much as pure water does, it now reads about one hundred and ninety-five millilitres. Look at the meters. Molarity has dropped: zero point one zero zero moles, over zero point one nine five litres. Zero point five one three. And molality? Zero point one zero zero moles, over zero point one eight zero zero kilograms. Still zero point five five six. It didn't move. We haven't removed any glucose. We haven't added any water. Yet the molarity changed. Molarity depends on volume, and volume depends on temperature. Molality depends on masses, and as long as nothing is lost or added, masses don't care. Cool it back down, and the volume shrinks. Molarity climbs back to zero point five two four. Same glucose. Same water. Two measures, behaving completely differently.
