[warm, conversational delivery] So which one should a chemist use? In a lab at steady room temperature, molarity is wonderfully practical. Measure a volume with a pipette, and you know exactly how many moles you've delivered. Titrations, dilutions, reaction yields. Volumes are quick to measure, so molarity rules the bench. [short pause] [thoughtful] But when temperature changes, or when we care about how solute and solvent particles share space, molality is the safer choice. Freezing points, boiling points, careful physical chemistry. [pause] [curious] And here's a detail worth seeing. For dilute solutions in water, the two numbers are close. A litre of a dilute solution is mostly water, and a litre of water weighs almost a kilogram. So moles per litre and moles per kilogram nearly match. As the solution gets stronger, they drift apart. The solute takes up room in the litre and adds to its mass, but adds nothing to the solvent's kilogram. [short pause] [thoughtful] Close is not the same. One molar is not one molal, and later we'll see exactly how density connects them.
