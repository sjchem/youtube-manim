Here is a glass of water. We stir in a spoonful of sugar. Glucose, to be exact. The crystals fall, dissolve, and spread out, until every part of the glass looks the same. Nothing unusual. Just a simple solution. Now, a question. How concentrated is it? One chemist says zero point five two four molar. Another says zero point five five six molal. A third works out a mole fraction of zero point zero zero nine nine zero. And a fourth reports nine point zero nine percent by mass. Four different numbers. Four different comparisons. One solution. So which one is right? All of them. Concentration isn't just about how much glucose is in the glass. It's about what we compare that glucose with. Change the comparison, and the number changes too, even though nothing in the glass has changed at all. Once you can see what each number is comparing, these formulas stop being things to memorise. They become things you can picture. So let's stop memorising, and start looking inside the solution.
