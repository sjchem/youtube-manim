During acid-base neutralisation, each mole of protons consumed corresponds to one mole of reaction equivalents. Count capacity for the specified reaction. Molarity counts the amount of specified solute. Normality counts its reaction equivalents per litre of solution. If each mole of acid supplies z equivalents for the stated reaction, normality is molarity times z. Here's the catch. How many protons does each molecule actually give up? It's tempting to say two. The sulfuric acid formula contains two acidic hydrogens, after all. But the molecule doesn't decide that on its own. The reaction does. So before we can state a normality, we have to name the reaction. Historically, normality was everywhere in analytical chemistry. You'll still meet it in older lab methods and textbooks. Today, the official recommendation is to avoid it, and to work with molarity and a balanced equation instead.
