Where does molality earn its keep? In properties that depend on how many solute particles share the solvent. Pure water freezes at zero degrees Celsius. To freeze, water molecules have to lock into an ordered crystal. Dissolved glucose dilutes the water, and that makes staying liquid slightly more favourable. The crystal only wins at a lower temperature. For a dilute solution, the drop is proportional to molality. Delta T equals K f times m. For water, K f is about one point eight six degrees per molal. Our solution: one point eight six times zero point five five six. About one point zero three degrees. It freezes near minus one degree Celsius. Boiling is shifted too, the other way, by a smaller amount: about three tenths of a degree, using a different constant. Why molality here, and not molarity? These effects are about the ratio of solute particles to solvent particles. Molality is built from exactly those two things: moles of solute, and a mass of solvent, which is a fixed number of solvent molecules. While the liquid keeps its composition, molality stays fixed as temperature changes. Once freezing or boiling removes solvent from the liquid, recalculate its molality.
