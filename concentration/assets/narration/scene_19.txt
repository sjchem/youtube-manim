Time to test yourself. Five statements. Each one sounds reasonable. Each one is a trap. Trap one. To make a one molar solution, dissolve one mole of solute in one litre of water. That does not fix the final solution volume. This glucose example expands; other mixtures may contract. Dissolve first, then make the complete solution up to one litre. Trap two. A one molar solution must also be one molal. Different denominators: a litre of solution versus a kilogram of solvent. Our glucose was zero point five two four molar, but zero point five five six molal. Trap three. The normality of sulfuric acid is always twice its molarity. Only if both protons react. Take one, and normality equals molarity. The factor belongs to the reaction, not the bottle. Trap four. Mole fraction is moles of solute, divided by moles of solvent. That's a mole ratio. Mole fraction divides by the total. For glucose: zero point zero one zero zero, versus zero point zero zero nine nine zero. Close, but different, and the gap grows in stronger solutions. Trap five. One ppm always means one milligram per litre. Only roughly, and only for water-like solutions. In ethanol, which is lighter than water, one milligram per litre is about one point two seven ppm by mass. Five traps, and one escape route: before you calculate, ask what's on top of the fraction, and what's on the bottom. Let's put everything we've seen on one map.
