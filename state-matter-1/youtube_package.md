## Description

Air looks like nothing, yet it presses on every square metre with the weight of
about ten tonnes. In this visual lesson we shrink into a gas and follow one
chain of reasoning: why molecules attract, why heat keeps them apart, how
countless collisions become pressure, and why Boyle's, Charles', Gay-Lussac's
and Avogadro's laws are four views of one equation, PV = nRT. We finish with
Dalton's law of partial pressures and the places where the ideal gas model
stops working.

Covers NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Unit 5 (States of Matter), §5.1–5.6:
intermolecular forces, thermal energy, the gas laws, the ideal gas equation,
density and molar mass, and Dalton's law. The kinetic-theory derivation of
PV = ⅓Nm⟨v²⟩ is included as enrichment.

The molecular demonstrations use reproducible models: the gas is a seeded ideal-gas simulation, the
solid–liquid–gas sequence is a real 2D Lennard-Jones simulation, with worked numbers computed from shared physical data. Everyday applications also use qualitative illustrations.
