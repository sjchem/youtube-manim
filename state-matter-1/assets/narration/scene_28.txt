Air is not one gas. It is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, sharing the same space. How does each gas contribute to the pressure? In an ideal mixture, molecules ignore each other. Each gas strikes the walls as if the others weren't there. So the total push on the walls is the sum of the pushes from each gas. P total equals P A plus P B. For any number of gases, the total is the sum of the partial pressures. Each partial pressure is what that gas alone would exert in the whole container: P i equals n i R T over V. So each gas's share of the pressure is its share of the molecules, its mole fraction, x i. P i equals x i times P total. Try it. Two moles of nitrogen and one mole of oxygen, at a total pressure of three bar. Nitrogen's mole fraction is two thirds, oxygen's one third. So nitrogen supplies two bar, and oxygen one. Back to the mountain. Oxygen is about twenty-one percent of air, so at sea level it supplies about zero point two one bar. On the summit of Everest, only about zero point zero seven. The same collisions that explained Boyle's law explain Dalton's.
