Now connect pressure to temperature. The average kinetic energy is one half m times the average of v squared. So one third N m times that average is two thirds N times the average kinetic energy. P V equals two thirds N times average K. And we already know the average kinetic energy: three halves k B T. Substitute, and the two thirds and the three halves cancel. P V equals N k B T. Finally, count molecules in moles. N is n times Avogadro's number, and Avogadro's number times k B is the gas constant, R. P V equals n R T. The ideal gas law, built from nothing but moving molecules and bouncing walls. Notice the difference. Temperature tracks the average energy of each molecule. Pressure also depends on how many molecules there are, and how much room they have. You won't be asked to derive this in Class eleven. But it shows why the gas laws work.
