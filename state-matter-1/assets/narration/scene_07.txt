If molecules attract, why don't they simply collapse into one another? Push two atoms closer and closer. At first, the attraction grows. But when their electron clouds begin to overlap, a fierce repulsion takes over. The electrons resist being squeezed into the same space, and the nuclei repel each other too. Put attraction and repulsion together, and you get the full interaction energy: a steep wall, a well, and a long, shallow tail. A common model is the Lennard-Jones curve. Its repulsive term rises as one over r to the twelfth. Its attractive term falls as one over r to the sixth. At the bottom of the well is the distance where the two are balanced. That is where neighbours in a liquid or a solid tend to settle. The depth of the well tells us how strongly molecules hold on to each other. So if they attract, why doesn't every gas collapse into a liquid?
