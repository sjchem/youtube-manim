This is the turning point of our story. Where does gas pressure come from? Start with just one molecule, of mass m, bouncing around inside a cube of side L. Focus on one wall, and on one part of the velocity: the part perpendicular to that wall, v x. The molecule strikes the wall and bounces back elastically. Its speed is unchanged. Its motion along the wall is unchanged. Only v x flips sign. Its momentum along x goes from plus m v x to minus m v x. A change of two m v x. That momentum doesn't vanish. The wall receives it: a push of two m v x, outward, every time. Take a nitrogen molecule whose speed perpendicular to the wall is about five hundred metres per second. Its momentum transfer is around five times ten to the minus twenty-three kilogram metres per second. Utterly tiny. So how can tiny pushes add up to the pressure of the air?
