Here is the puzzle at the heart of this lesson. Up close, everything looks random. Follow one molecule: repeated collisions change its direction. Tracking every molecule in a real gas is impractical. Step back, and the randomness disappears. The pressure gauge holds steady. So does the thermometer. Pressure, volume and temperature can be measured, and predicted. The same thing happens with a crowd of people. You can't predict where one person in a busy station will walk next. But you can predict how many will pass through the gates each minute. The gas laws describe the crowd, not the individual. To understand them, we will follow one chain of reasoning, in four questions. What holds molecules together, and what lets them separate? Why does a gas push on its container? Why do pressure, volume and temperature follow simple rules? And can one equation explain them all? Let's begin with the forces between molecules.
