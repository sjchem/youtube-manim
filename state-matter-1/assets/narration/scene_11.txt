Two things matter: how big each push is, and how often it comes. After hitting the wall, our molecule must cross the box and come back before it can strike again: a distance of two L, at speed v x. So the time between hits is two L over v x. On a graph of force against time, each hit is a sharp spike. Its area is the momentum delivered: two m v x. Now spread each spike evenly over the time until the next one. The area stays the same, but the force becomes steady. Average force equals the push, divided by the time between pushes: two m v x, over two L over v x. The twos cancel, and one v x moves up. The average force is m v x squared over L. Faster molecules push harder, and they come back sooner. That's why the speed appears squared.
