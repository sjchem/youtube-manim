[warm, conversational delivery] We now have four laws. Each one holds two quantities constant, and relates the other two. Boyle: constant temperature and amount. V is proportional to one over P. Charles: constant pressure and amount. V is proportional to T. Gay-Lussac: constant volume and amount. P is proportional to T. Avogadro: constant pressure and temperature. V is proportional to n. Combine the volume relations, and V is proportional to n T over P. Replace the proportionality with a constant, R, and rearrange. [short pause] [emphasized] P V equals n R T. The ideal gas equation. [pause] [curious] Now picture every state of one mole of ideal gas, all at once. Pressure, volume and temperature form a single curved surface. Slice it at constant temperature, and you get Boyle's hyperbola. Slice it at constant volume, and you get Gay-Lussac's straight line. Slice it at constant pressure, and you get Charles' line. [pause] [warmly] The four laws were never separate. They are views of one surface, one equation.
