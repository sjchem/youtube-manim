[warm, conversational delivery] Boyle's law hides a second idea. Squeeze a gas into half the space, and the same molecules are packed twice as densely. At constant temperature, density is proportional to pressure. [short pause] You can feel this on a mountain. Air at sea level is squeezed by the weight of all the air above it. Climb, and there is less air above you, so the pressure falls, and the air thins out. On the summit of Everest, the pressure is only about a third of its value at sea level. Every breath holds far fewer oxygen molecules. That's why climbers struggle, and why many carry oxygen. [pause] One more warning. Boyle's law needs constant temperature. Pump a bicycle tyre quickly, and the pump gets warm. Rapid compression does work on the gas faster than heat can escape. The molecules speed up, and the pressure rises more than Boyle's law predicts. [short pause] [emphasized] So before using any gas law, ask first: what is being held constant?
