[warm, conversational delivery] In eighteen eleven, Amedeo Avogadro took the idea further. Take three boxes of the same volume, at the same temperature and pressure: one of helium, one of nitrogen, one of carbon dioxide. Helium atoms are light and fast. Carbon dioxide molecules are heavy and slow. But at the same temperature, they all carry the same average kinetic energy. Light particles hit more often but transfer less momentum per hit. At the same temperature, their time-averaged pressure contribution per particle is the same. Equal pressures and volumes therefore mean equal particle numbers. [short pause] [emphasized] Equal volumes of ideal gases, at the same temperature and pressure, contain equal numbers of molecules. Whatever the gas. How many? At room temperature and one bar, every litre holds about two point four times ten to the twenty-two molecules. This gives a universal molar volume. At two hundred and seventy-three point one five kelvin and one bar, one mole of any ideal gas occupies twenty-two point seven one litres. [pause] You may have seen twenty-two point four litres. That value belongs to one atmosphere, a slightly higher pressure than one bar.
