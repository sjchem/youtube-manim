[pause] [curious] Now a particularly important, directional attraction: the hydrogen bond. When hydrogen is bonded to a very electronegative atom, such as oxygen, nitrogen or fluorine, electron density shifts away from hydrogen. It carries a partial positive charge, not a full positive charge. That spot is drawn to a lone pair on a neighbouring molecule. In water, each molecule can join up to four of these links, building a shifting three dimensional network. Ammonia forms them too, through the lone pair on its nitrogen. [short pause] [emphasized] The effect is dramatic. Hydrogen sulfide, a heavier molecule with a similar shape, boils at about minus sixty degrees Celsius. Water, with its hydrogen bonds, boils at one hundred. Hydrogen bonds hold liquid water together, and explain many of its unusual properties.
