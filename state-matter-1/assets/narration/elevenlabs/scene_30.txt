[warm, conversational delivery] We began with an enormous crowd of molecules, moving randomly inside a container. From their collisions, we explained pressure. From pressure, temperature, volume and amount, we found the gas laws, and one equation that unites them. [pause] [emphasized] But our model hides a simplification. We treated molecules as points that never attract one another. Real molecules attract. A molecule about to strike the wall is tugged back by its neighbours, so it hits a little softer. Real molecules take up space. Squeeze a gas hard enough, and that space is no longer negligible. Even an ideal gas already has a spread of molecular speeds: the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. Understanding that spread is our next step. [pause] [curious] So what happens when these assumptions fail? That is where the ideal gas ends, and real gases begin. See you in Part two.
