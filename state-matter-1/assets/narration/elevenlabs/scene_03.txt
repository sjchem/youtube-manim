[warm, conversational delivery] Start with a single water molecule. Its oxygen and two hydrogens are held together by covalent bonds. These are forces inside the molecule: intramolecular forces. But a glass of water holds trillions of trillions of molecules, and they cling to one another. Forces between molecules are called intermolecular forces. [short pause] [emphasized] For water, these intermolecular links are much weaker. Breaking an O H bond inside water takes about four hundred and sixty kilojoules per mole. A typical attraction between neighbouring water molecules is only about twenty. Boiling water doesn't break water molecules apart. It only pulls whole molecules away from their neighbours. [pause] [curious] So where does an attraction between neutral molecules come from? There are several mechanisms. And the simplest one works even for atoms with no polarity at all.
