[warm, conversational delivery] Put the two experiments side by side. In both, heating does the same thing to the molecules: it raises their average kinetic energy. With a free piston, the gas expands, and the pressure stays the same. With rigid walls, the volume stays the same, and the pressure rises. [short pause] [emphasized] One microscopic change. Two macroscopic outcomes. The difference is only what we hold constant. [pause] Car tyres show this, roughly. After a long drive, the air inside is warmer, and the pressure reads higher. A tyre can stretch a little, so it isn't perfectly rigid, but Gay-Lussac's law gives a good estimate. Constant pressure: V is proportional to T. Constant volume: P is proportional to T.
