[warm, conversational delivery] Here's where Charles' law reveals something deep. Measure a gas's volume at several temperatures in Celsius, and plot the points. They fall on a straight line. Repeat with a different amount of gas, or at a different pressure. Another straight line, with a different slope. Now extend every line backward, toward colder temperatures. [short pause] [emphasized] They all meet at the same point: zero volume, at minus two hundred and seventy-three point one five degrees Celsius. That temperature is absolute zero. Measuring from there gives the Kelvin scale: T in kelvin equals the Celsius temperature plus two hundred and seventy-three point one five. On the Kelvin scale, every line passes through the origin. That's why gas laws need absolute temperature. [pause] One caution. No real gas reaches zero volume. Real gases leave the ideal-gas regime, and many condense on cooling. The meeting point is an extrapolation of ideal gas behaviour, not a measurement.
