[warm, conversational delivery] Because molecules never sit still. Gas particles move continuously. Molecules can also rotate and vibrate. Here, we focus on their translational motion through the container. Temperature is related to average energy per particle, rather than the total energy of the sample. For a classical ideal gas, average translational kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature. [short pause] [emphasized] Average K equals three halves k B T. Here k B is the Boltzmann constant, about one point three eight times ten to the minus twenty-three joules per kelvin, and T is measured in kelvin. [short pause] Notice the word average. At any instant, some molecules are slow and some are fast. Temperature describes the whole distribution, not any one molecule. Heat the gas, and the whole distribution shifts to higher speeds. At room temperature, the average is tiny: about six times ten to the minus twenty-one joules per molecule. But per mole, the thermal energy scale, R T, is about two and a half kilojoules.
