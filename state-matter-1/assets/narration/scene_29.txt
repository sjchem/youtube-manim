Dalton's law turns up in every chemistry lab. Collect a gas, say hydrogen, by bubbling it into an upturned jar of water. The gas in the jar also contains water vapour. Once it is saturated at the measured temperature, water contributes its equilibrium vapour pressure. Equal water levels inside and outside make the total gas pressure equal atmospheric pressure. So the measured pressure is the dry gas plus water vapour. P dry gas equals P total minus the pressure of water vapour. At twenty-five degrees Celsius, water vapour supplies about zero point zero three one seven bar. If the total is one bar, the dry gas exerts zero point nine six eight bar. This vapour pressure of water is called aqueous tension, and it rises with temperature.
