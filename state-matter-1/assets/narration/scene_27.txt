The ideal gas equation can even weigh molecules. Write the amount as mass over molar mass. Then P V equals m over M, times R T, and the density, m over V, equals P M over R T. At the same pressure and temperature, density is proportional to molar mass. At one bar and three hundred kelvin, nitrogen has a density of about one point one two grams per litre. Helium, about zero point one six. Seven times lighter. That's why a helium balloon rises. Turn it around, and a density measurement gives the molar mass: M equals density times R T over P. One point one two grams per litre gives twenty-eight grams per mole: nitrogen. But remember what this equation assumes: molecules with no size, and no attractions. Hold on to that thought.
