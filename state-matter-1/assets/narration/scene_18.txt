Charles' question: what happens when you heat a gas, but keep its pressure constant? Put a freely sliding piston on top, loaded with a weight. The load and atmosphere set the external pressure. At equilibrium, the gas pressure balances that load. Now heat the gas. The molecules speed up. They strike harder and more often. For a moment, the pressure inside beats the weight, and the piston rises. As the volume grows, the molecules have farther to travel. Hits on each square centimetre become less frequent, until the push balances the weight again. Higher temperature, same pressure, larger volume. Measure carefully, and the volume grows in step with absolute temperature. Double the kelvin temperature, and the volume doubles. V is proportional to T. V one over T one equals V two over T two. A hot air balloon shows a related effect, rather than a fixed-amount Charles experiment. Air escapes through its open bottom. The warmer air inside is less dense, and the buoyant force can lift the balloon.
