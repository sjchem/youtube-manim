One variable left: the amount of gas. Keep the temperature constant, with a weighted piston setting the external pressure. Compare states after the piston has balanced. Then add more molecules. More molecules means more hits on the walls each second. The pressure inside rises above the weight, and the piston rises. It stops when the molecules are spread just as thinly as before: the same number in each litre, the same hits on each square centimetre. Double the molecules, double the volume. V is proportional to n. V one over n one equals V two over n two. So a container with twice as many molecules doesn't have to be at twice the pressure. If its volume doubles, the pressure doesn't change at all. You see a related effect when breathing in. Your chest expands first, lowering lung pressure slightly so air flows in. Near the same pressure and temperature, more air occupies more volume.
