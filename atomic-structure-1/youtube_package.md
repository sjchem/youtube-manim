## Description

A familiar drawing of an atom hides a problem: a classical orbiting electron
should lose energy and spiral into the nucleus. So why are atoms stable?

We're not going to memorize the models of the atom. We're going to discover why
each one had to exist. Follow the evidence from a beam inside a glass tube, to
Rutherford's gold foil, to the barcode in hydrogen's light.

Along the way, imagine a cricket-ball nucleus with an atom stretching about five
kilometres from its centre. This is an approximate scale comparison; the exact
ratio depends on the atom and the radius definition.
