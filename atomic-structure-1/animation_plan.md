# Animation plan — fifteen scenes

Planned duration: **22:05**. The audience first feels the problem, then hears the
promise, then faces the bigger mystery, and only then begins the investigation.
No list of names or syllabus preview interrupts the opening.

| New scene | Previous scene(s) | Editorial reason |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | 01 + 02 | Collapse and promise belong to one opening |
| 02 | 03 | Keep the inference analogy short and clear |
| 03 | 04 | Establish Dalton through a chemical recipe |
| 04 | 05 | Let the electron experiment breathe |
| 05 | 06 + 07 | A model leads directly into its testable prediction |
| 06 | 08 | Preserve gold foil as the central surprise |
| 07 | 09 | Give the nucleus and scale comparison their own payoff |
| 08 | 10 | Return to the opening instability question |
| 09 | 11 + 12 | Keep the prism and change its source |
| 10 | 13 | Separate allowed energies from transitions |
| 11 | 14 | Connect each energy gap directly to its colour |
| 12 | 15 | Evaluate what the model explains |
| 13 | 16 | Distinguish the Bohr picture from modern physics |
| 14 | 17 | Keep the next video explanation and quantum cliffhanger together |
| 15 | 18 | Give the subscription call its own closing card |

The chapter functions are merged at source level. There is no internal scene
end/begin boundary inside the three merged sequences. FullVideo and the CLI share
one registry. All scene numbers below refer to the revised sequence.

Camera sweeps ease in and out; 3D captions stay fixed to the frame. Flat diagrams
and text remain steady while the voice explains. Camera orientation resets after
3D sequences. A blank chapter fade is only the short transition to the next scene.

## 01 · The Impossible Atom · 0:00–1:05

3D orbit → radiation → inward spiral → collapse. Replace explanatory sentence cards with a concealed object inside four inspection brackets and a drawn question mark. Keep the exact promise in narration only. Three probe paths disappear behind the object and produce visible detector flashes; a gentle scan leaves its interior unresolved. Fade the question and evidence into a centered closing title, “How We Discovered What an Atom Looks Like,” held for four seconds within the existing 65-second window. No written promise, timeline, model labels, or orbital reveal.

## 02 · Seeing the Invisible · 1:05–2:15

Dark-room paths miss, rebound and gradually constrain an outline. The narration explains that real particles can turn through electric forces without touching a surface. Every path is evidence.

## 03 · Dalton's Starting Point · 2:15–3:30

Open with John Dalton's portrait and name beside the shaded 3D cube; fade the portrait as the first cutting planes appear. Split the cube along three perpendicular planes into eight actual pieces. Separate the pieces and follow one with a smooth magnifying move; repeat once. Reveal a luminous opaque sphere with a soft corona and surface shading that makes its continuous rotation visible. Keep the sphere rotating while the flat water-recipe diagram explains two hydrogen atoms for one oxygen atom, then introduce the electrical clue. This remains Dalton's solid model, without a nucleus or electron orbits. See [portrait credits](assets/images/README.md).

## 04 · Something Smaller Than an Atom · 3:30–5:30

Assemble a 3D glass envelope on a wooden base, metal cathode, perforated copper anode and fluorescent screen. Use a gentle opening camera move, small component labels and no explanatory sentence captions or vacuum label. Keep the rays invisible: only the screen spot and obstacle's shadow appear. Move the spot up and down when the actual plate polarity signs reverse. This is a teaching reconstruction combining shadow and deflection demonstrations, not a replica of one historical tube. Clear the apparatus for a public-domain portrait of J. J. Thomson alongside the charge-to-mass result; retain the explicit modern electron-mass comparison. Preserve the final “CHANGE EVERYTHING. RUN IT AGAIN.” trial sequence. Portrait source and licensing: [image credits](assets/images/README.md).

## 05 · Thomson's Model and Its Prediction · 5:30–7:30

Let the opening title fade after the electrons appear. Balance the six illustrated electrons with a large orange +6e term and a cyan −6e term, each with a short positive/negative label; the example count does not specify an element. Introduce the positive cloud and electrons with matching colored labels, then clear both labels and the model title before the extended camera orbit. Fade the probe and prediction headings as their animations take over. Show the bend-exaggeration note before the illustrative trajectory. Introduce a compact five-row sheet with its caption above the atoms, then remove the caption before seven separated particle paths cross it. Preserve the final prediction card and arrow animation.

## 06 · The One That Came Back · 7:30–9:35

Introduce the apparatus with a brief “EXPERIMENT” title. Fade the title while it is still pinned to the screen, before releasing it from the camera. Use a thin metallic gold sheet with warm highlights, a subdued detector ring and an animated particle reaching a detector flash. Let the camera move smoothly to the overhead view. The narration explains the detector geometry and the selected trajectories; omit sentence captions over the apparatus. Fade old trails as new particles arrive, and dim old flashes before the rare return. Pulse the detector at the actual backward impact point. Clear the apparatus before showing “≈ 1 in 20,000” on its own card with “Textbook example”; narration retains the conditions and sampling qualifications. Finish with the conflict between the observed deflections and Thomson's diffuse charge.

## 07 · A Cricket Ball and Five Kilometres · 9:35–11:30

Animate scattering beside one brief cue at a time: mostly open space, positive core, tiny target. Carry the atom into the nucleus explanation, then reveal large bright atomic and nuclear radius values in sequence. Replace the constructed ball with a public-domain cricket-ball photograph; briefly introduce the analogy, clear its caption, and shrink the photo smoothly into the schematic centre marker. Animate a walk across a quiet street-grid motif with only “About 5 km / Centre to edge.” Keep the ten-kilometre diameter and scale qualifications in narration. Fade the boundary as its meaning is explained. End on electrons and field arrows, with a brief cue that clears before the visual hold.

## 08 · The Beautiful Problem · 11:30–12:50

Compare gravitational and electrical orbits. Use large, bright cyan velocity and pale-gold acceleration symbols matching the moving arrows. Give the radiation statement bold, high-contrast lettering, with bright energy-loss and shrinking-radius terms above the diagram. Clear the vector labels and radiation statement when collapse starts. Match the spiral to the electron’s current position and orbit radius. Clear the diagram before the centred lifetime and failure conclusion, then preserve the final question.

## 09 · The Barcode in the Light · 12:50–14:55

Briefly introduce light from atoms, then clear the heading. Show a power supply, current reaching the gas, the discharge turning on and coloured light packets leaving the tube. Use one smooth wave that contracts from red through green to violet, with a crest-to-crest wavelength bracket that contracts with it. Clear the short wave cues before the optical experiment. Keep the prism and vertical screen fixed as white light produces a continuous fan, then hydrogen produces four separate rays and matching lines. Rotate/morph this measured spectrum into a horizontal barcode, ask a brief question, and relight the same positions. Compare hydrogen and selected helium lines with a moving scan. A star and small common wavelength shift illustrate the narration’s astronomical example; intensity changes leave positions unchanged. Hand off visually to an illustrative staircase, with no long closing sentence. The main explanations and spectrum qualifications remain in narration.

## 10 · Energy Has Floors · 14:55–16:30

Show a shaded ramp and a plain gold ball, then replace the slope with solid steps with lit top faces. Let the ball settle on successive treads; remove explanatory captions. Fade the physical faces away and stretch the treads into an abstract energy diagram, leaving the analogy qualification to narration. Respace the rails using hydrogen's real relative energies. Briefly introduce HYDROGEN, then clear it while retaining the main level numbers and the original GROUND STATE label. Show upper levels converging without a sentence caption. A quiet clock accompanies a stationary ground-state electron. An upward energy arrow connects allowed end states; a later n=3 to n=2 transition emits a red light packet. Keep the explanations in the unchanged narration.

## 11 · A Jump Becomes a Colour · 16:30–18:05

Keep an energy-proportional Balmer ladder at left, a large ΔE = hν card above the right column, and separated wave rows below it. Remove the persistent title and sentence captions. Show the energy gap with a bright ΔE bracket. Compare the visible 3→2 and 6→2 photons with wavelength-proportional wave spacing and equal displayed speeds. For each of four Balmer transitions, light a spectral line and clear its arrow and photon before continuing. Clear the ladder before the 5→2 numerical example (ΔE ≈ 2.86 eV, λ ≈ 434 nm). End on the equation and barcode, with no arrows or sentences left over. Narration explains that arrows represent energy changes, not filmed spatial trajectories.

## 12 · What Bohr Got Right · 18:05–19:30

Compare the same questions for Rutherford and Bohr. Preserve the nucleus and explain what stationary energies add. Display the compact energy formula and values, then return to the one idea: quantized energies.

## 13 · Why an Orbit Is Not Enough · 19:30–20:50

Briefly ask whether circular tracks are literal, then clear the question before magnifying the atom. Place “no.” below the smaller atom, preserving transparent rings while fading it. Present multi-electron and finer spectral limits in smaller, warm cream text. Remove the “deeper problem” lead-in and retain the wave-behaviour statement. Dissolve the track into a labelled wave analogy without claiming that electrons stop having particle-like behaviour.

## 14 · Beyond the Orbit · 20:50–21:50

Recap over an enlarged orbit. Briefly ask what replaces the circular path, then fade the question while it is still fixed to the screen. Dissolve the electron into a brighter, denser 3D probability cloud whose dots face the camera. Briefly show ‘One electron’; leave the longer probability explanation in narration. Fade the cloud completely before resetting the camera. Close on NEXT VIDEO: Why Electrons Don't Orbit the Nucleus, with the next mystery of a stable quantum atom. The subscribe call follows separately.

## 15 · Subscribe: Stay Curious · 21:50–22:05

A separate flat closing card using Scene_15_Subscribe. Fade in THANK YOU FOR WATCHING, then the gold SUBSCRIBE headline and cyan rule, then Stay curious and Keep following physics, chemistry and mathematics. Give the headline one gentle emphasis, and hold the complete card beneath its own narration.
